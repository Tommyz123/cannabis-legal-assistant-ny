# Labor Rights and Employer Obligations
## NY Labor Law Section 201-d: Cannabis Use and Employment Rights

## Regulatory Overview

### Core Legal Basis
- **NY Labor Law Section 201-d** - Prohibition of discrimination based on lawful recreational activities
- **Marijuana Regulation and Taxation Act (MRTA)** - Marijuana Regulation and Taxation Act (effective March 31, 2021)
- **NYS DOL Guidance on Adult Use Cannabis and the Workplace** - New York State Department of Labor official workplace guidance

---

## I. Employee Protected Rights

### 1.1 Off-Duty Use Protection

Under **NY Labor Law § 201-d**, employers are **prohibited** from taking adverse action against employees for:

- ✅ Using cannabis during **off-duty hours**
- ✅ Using cannabis **outside employer premises**
- ✅ Using cannabis **without using employer equipment or property**

**Protected Actions Include:**
- Refusal to hire
- Termination
- Demotion
- Any other form of employment discrimination

### 1.2 Remote Work Special Provisions

**Important Provision:** The New York State Department of Labor explicitly states that an employee's **private residence used for remote work is not considered a "workplace"**.

**This Means:**
- If employees use cannabis during off-duty hours while working remotely at home, they remain protected under § 201-d
- Employers cannot take action against employees for using cannabis at home during off-duty hours

### 1.3 Non-Waivable Rights

Employers **cannot** require employees to:
- Waive § 201-d rights as a condition of employment
- Waive these rights as a condition of continued employment

## II. Employer Rights and Boundary Limitations

### 2.1 What Employers Can Do

#### ✅ Workplace Prohibition Policies

Employers have **broad discretion** to prohibit cannabis use in the following situations:

1. **During workplace hours** - Regardless of whether during work time
2. **During work hours** - Regardless of whether at workplace
3. **When using employer equipment or property**

**Scenario Example 1: Warehouse Worker**
- Situation: Employee smokes cannabis in parking lot during lunch break
- Employer Action: Can prohibit and discipline
- Reason: Even during non-work time, still on employer premises

**Scenario Example 2: Truck Driver**
- Situation: Employee uses cannabis while driving company vehicle
- Employer Action: Can terminate immediately
- Reason: Using employer property (company vehicle) and involves safety issues

#### ✅ Actions Based on Impaired Performance

When employees exhibit **specific articulable symptoms** while at work, employers can take action.

**Legal Standard:**
Impairment means the employee exhibits specific articulable symptoms at work that:
1. **Decrease or reduce** employee's ability to perform job duties, **or**
2. **Interfere with** employer's obligation to provide safe and healthy work environment (complying with state and federal occupational safety and health law requirements)

**Examples of Symptoms That May Constitute Impairment:**
- Operating heavy machinery in unsafe and reckless manner
- Slurred speech
- Poor coordination or difficulty operating company equipment
- Glassy eyes, lack of focus, lack of coordination
- Significantly delayed reaction time
- Inability to complete normal work tasks

**Scenario Example 3: Forklift Operator**
- Situation: Employee repeatedly hits shelves while operating forklift, shows uncoordinated movements, delayed reactions
- Employer Action: Can remove employee from position, require medical evaluation
- Basis: Exhibits specific articulable safety hazard symptoms

### 2.2 What Employers Cannot Do

#### ❌ Drug Testing Limitations

Employers **cannot** require employees or job applicants to submit to cannabis testing, **unless**:

**Exceptions:**

1. **Required by federal or state law**
   - Example: Department of Transportation (DOT) regulated positions
   - Example: Specific positions for federal contractors

2. **To avoid violating federal law**
   - Not testing would result in violation of federal law
   - Not testing would result in loss of federal contract or federal funding

3. **Employee exhibits specific impairment symptoms**
   - Must be objectively observable current signs of impairment
   - Complies with above "specific articulable symptoms" standard

**Scenario Example 4: Retail Clerk (Incorrect Practice)**
- Situation: Employer conducts pre-employment cannabis testing on all new employees
- Issue: Violates § 201-d unless it falls under exceptions
- Consequence: Employee can file discrimination lawsuit

**Scenario Example 5: IT Technician (Incorrect Practice)**
- Situation: Manager smells cannabis odor on employee, requires drug test
- Issue: Odor alone does not constitute "specific articulable impairment symptoms"
- Result: Cannot require drug test based on odor

#### ❌ Cannot Be Used as Evidence of Impairment

The following situations **explicitly cannot** be used alone as evidence of impairment:

1. **Cannabis Odor**
   - Even if employee has cannabis odor, cannot be used alone as evidence of impairment
   - Cannot require drug test or take disciplinary action based solely on odor

2. **Positive Drug Test Results**
   - Positive cannabis test **cannot** prove employee is currently impaired
   - THC can remain in body for weeks, does not reflect current impairment status

**Scenario Example 6: Office Worker**
- Situation: Employee arrives at work Monday, manager smells cannabis odor
- Employee Explanation: Used at home over weekend, clothing has residual odor
- Employer Action Limitation: Cannot terminate or drug test based solely on odor
- Required: Must have objective evidence of actual work performance decline

## III. Workplace Policy Requirements

### 3.1 Policy Update Recommendations

Employers should update employee handbooks and policies to clearly:

1. **Prohibited Locations and Times**
   - Clearly define "workplace"
   - Clarify "work hours" scope
   - Explain policy for remote work

2. **Impairment Standards**
   - List specific behavior examples that may constitute impairment
   - Explain how employer evaluates impairment
   - Clarify consequences in case of impairment

3. **Safety-Sensitive Positions**
   - Identify safety-sensitive or federally regulated positions
   - Explain special requirements for these positions
   - Explain applicable exceptions

### 3.2 Policy Implementation Best Practices

1. **Train Management**
   - How to identify objective impairment symptoms
   - How to distinguish odor from actual impairment
   - How to document observed symptoms

2. **Consistent Enforcement**
   - Apply policy equally to all employees
   - Avoid selective enforcement
   - Maintain detailed records

3. **Reasonable Accommodation**
   - Consider employee medical cannabis situations
   - Evaluate if reasonable accommodation can be provided
   - Document interactive process

**Scenario Example 7: Construction Site Supervisor (Best Practice)**
- Situation: Supervisor notices worker behaving abnormally
- Steps:
  1. Document specific observations: unsteady movements, slow reactions, unable to properly use tools
  2. Remove worker from operational position
  3. Have private conversation, ask if help needed
  4. Take appropriate action according to company policy
  5. Document entire process
- Result: Complies with § 201-d requirements while maintaining workplace safety

## IV. Detailed Drug Testing Policy Limitations

### 4.1 Prohibited Testing Scenarios

❌ **Pre-Employment Testing** - Unless position falls under exceptions
❌ **Random Testing** - Unless required by federal law
❌ **Odor-Based Testing** - Odor is not sufficient reason
❌ **Follow-up Testing Based Solely on Positive Test** - Requires new impairment symptoms

### 4.2 Permitted Testing Scenarios

✅ **DOT Regulated Positions** - Required by federal law
✅ **Specific Federal Contractor Positions** - To avoid loss of federal funding
✅ **Based on Specific Impairment Symptoms** - With objective observable evidence
✅ **Reasonable Suspicion Testing** - Based on specific behavior and performance issues

**Scenario Example 8: Freight Company (Legal Testing)**
- Position: Commercial Driver (CDL)
- Testing Requirement: DOT-required pre-employment and random drug testing
- Status: Legal, falls under federal law requirement exception

**Scenario Example 9: Accounting Firm (Illegal Testing)**
- Position: Regular accounting employee
- Testing Program: Annual random drug testing for all employees
- Status: Violates § 201-d unless there are impairment symptoms

## V. Employee Rights Remedies

### 5.1 Violation Reporting

If employer violates § 201-d, employees can:

1. **File Complaint with New York State Department of Labor**
   - Website: dol.ny.gov
   - Phone: (888) 469-7365

2. **File Civil Lawsuit**
   - Can seek damages
   - Can seek reinstatement
   - Can seek attorney fees

3. **File Complaint with New York State Division of Human Rights**
   - If other discrimination factors involved

### 5.2 Protection Period

- Complaints and lawsuits have statute of limitations
- Recommended to preserve relevant evidence promptly
- Consult employment attorney to understand rights

## VI. Special Situation Handling

### 6.1 Medical Cannabis Patients

While § 201-d protects recreational use, medical cannabis patients may have additional protections:

- **New York State Compassionate Care Act** may provide additional protections
- Employers may need to provide reasonable accommodation (unless causing undue hardship)
- Intersection with disability law considerations

**Scenario Example 10: Chronic Pain Patient**
- Situation: Employee has medical cannabis prescription for chronic back pain treatment
- Work: Data entry clerk (non-safety sensitive)
- Employer Obligation: May need to consider reasonable accommodation
- Note: Still cannot use during work hours or at workplace

### 6.2 Multi-State Employers

- Laws vary greatly between states
- New York employers must comply with New York law, even if headquarters in other state
- Need to develop state-specific policies

### 6.3 Federal Contractors

- Federal drug-free workplace policy may require stricter standards
- Need to consult legal counsel to balance state and federal requirements
- May need position-specific policies

## VII. Key Takeaways Summary

### Core Messages for Employers

1. **Default Rule: Cannot Discriminate** - Employees' off-duty legal cannabis use is protected
2. **Workplace Prohibition: Can Enforce** - Can prohibit use at workplace, during work hours
3. **Drug Testing: Highly Restricted** - Only in exceptional circumstances
4. **Impairment: Need Evidence** - Must be objectively observable specific symptoms, odor and positive tests insufficient
5. **Remote Work: Employee Home = Protected Zone** - Off-duty use at employee home is protected

### Core Messages for Employees

1. **Protected: Off-Duty Use** - Using cannabis at home during off-duty hours is protected
2. **Not Protected: During Work** - Use at workplace or during work hours not protected
3. **Drug Testing Limited: Employers Cannot Test Arbitrarily** - Unless federally required or impairment symptoms
4. **Odor ≠ Impairment** - Employer cannot take action based solely on odor
5. **Remedies Available** - If rights violated, can file complaints and lawsuits

## X. WARN Act Requirements

### 10.1 Federal WARN Act Overview

The **Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification (WARN) Act** (29 U.S.C. § 2101 et seq.) requires covered employers to provide **60 days advance written notice** before plant closings or mass layoffs.

**Federal WARN Applicability:**
- Applies to employers with **100 or more full-time employees**
- Counts employees at a single employment site

**Triggering Events:**
- **Plant Closing:** Permanent or temporary shutdown of a single site affecting **50 or more employees** during any 30-day period
- **Mass Layoff:** Employment loss for **500 or more employees** at a single site, OR **50–499 employees** if that represents **33% or more** of the active workforce

**Required Notice Recipients:**
1. Affected employees (or their union representatives)
2. New York State Department of Labor
3. Chief elected official of the unit of local government

**Penalties for Non-Compliance:**
- Back pay and benefits for each day of violation, up to **60 days**
- Civil penalty of up to **$500 per day** of violation payable to the relevant unit of local government
- Court costs and attorneys' fees

---

### 10.2 New York State WARN Act (NY Labor Law § 860)

New York's WARN Act is **significantly more expansive** than the federal version.

**Key Differences from Federal WARN:**

| Provision | Federal WARN | NY WARN |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| Employer threshold | 100+ full-time employees | **50+ full-time employees** |
| Notice period | 60 days | **90 days** |
| Plant closing threshold | 50+ employees | **25+ employees** |
| Mass layoff threshold | 500+ OR 50+ (33%) | **25+ employees (33% of workforce)** |
| Relocation coverage | Not covered | **Relocations of 50+ miles affecting 25+ workers** |

**Additional NY WARN Requirements:**
- Notice must be sent to the **NYS Commissioner of Labor**
- Notice must be sent to the **local workforce investment board**
- Notice must specify: reason for layoff, expected date, number and job titles affected, bumping rights information

**Cannabis Industry Application:**
- Most dispensaries have fewer than 50 employees and are **not covered** by either WARN Act
- Multi-location operators or large cannabis companies may exceed thresholds
- During expansion or acquisition: assess combined employee counts
- Always consult legal counsel before any significant workforce reduction

---

### 10.3 WARN Act Compliance Checklist for Cannabis Operators

- [ ] Determine total full-time employee count across all locations
- [ ] If 50+ employees: track NY WARN thresholds
- [ ] If 100+ employees: track Federal WARN thresholds
- [ ] Maintain current employee count records by site
- [ ] Establish internal protocol for advance notice when reducing workforce
- [ ] Designate HR contact responsible for WARN compliance
- [ ] Review before any merger, acquisition, or license surrender

## XI. Minimum Wage and Pay Requirements

### 11.1 New York State Minimum Wage

Cannabis dispensary employees are entitled to New York's minimum wage, which **increases annually on January 1**.

**Current Rates (Effective January 1, 2025):**

| Region | Minimum Wage (2025) |
|--------|---------------------|
| New York City | **$16.50/hour** |
| Long Island (Nassau, Suffolk Counties) | **$16.50/hour** |
| Westchester County | **$16.50/hour** |
| Remainder of New York State | **$15.50/hour** |

> **Note:** NYC Admin Code may impose additional local requirements. Verify current rates with the NYS Department of Labor at dol.ny.gov/minimum-wage.

**Tipped Employees:**
- Cannabis retail employees who receive tips are typically classified as **non-tipped service workers** under NY law
- Standard minimum wage rates apply (not reduced tip credit rates)
- Dispensary owners should consult legal counsel before implementing tip policies

### 11.2 Overtime Requirements

- **Federal FLSA:** 1.5× regular rate for hours over 40 per workweek
- **New York Labor Law:** Same 40-hour threshold
- **Exemptions:** Administrative, executive, and professional exemptions apply; most budtenders and floor staff are **non-exempt**

### 11.3 Spread-of-Hours Pay

Under **12 NYCRR § 146-1.6**, employees in New York must receive one additional hour of pay at the minimum wage rate for any day their spread of hours exceeds **10 hours** (including meal breaks). This commonly applies to cannabis retail workers on extended shifts.

### 11.4 Pay Frequency and Wage Theft Prevention Act

- **NY Labor Law § 191:** Manual workers (including most dispensary floor staff) must be paid **weekly**
- **Wage Theft Prevention Act:** Employers must provide written wage notice at hire and update upon any pay rate change
- **Pay stubs required:** Must itemize: gross wages, deductions, net pay, pay period dates, employer information, and employee's regular/overtime rate

### 11.5 Workers' Compensation

- All cannabis employees **must be covered** under NY Workers' Compensation Law
- Employers cannot misclassify dispensary workers as independent contractors to avoid coverage
- Must carry NYS-compliant workers' compensation insurance **before** first employee starts
- Penalties for non-coverage: stop-work orders, civil penalties, criminal charges
- Coverage required for: full-time, part-time, seasonal, and family employees

## XII. Cannabis Labor Peace Agreement

### 12.1 Requirement Under MRTA

The **Marijuana Regulation and Taxation Act (MRTA)** requires cannabis license applicants with **10 or more employees** to demonstrate a good-faith effort to enter into a **labor peace agreement** with a bona fide labor organization as a **condition of licensure** (Cannabis Law § 61).

**This requirement applies to:**
- Adult-Use Retail Dispensary licenses
- Microbusiness licenses
- Delivery licenses
- Other adult-use licenses where the licensee has 10+ employees

**Definition of Labor Peace Agreement:**
An agreement between the cannabis employer and a **labor organization** (union) in which:
- The **employer** agrees not to lock out employees and not to use permanent replacement workers during a strike
- The **labor organization** agrees not to strike, picket, boycott, or engage in concerted action that disrupts the employer's operations
- Both parties agree to a neutral dispute resolution mechanism

### 12.2 Application Requirements

When applying for or renewing a cannabis license, the applicant must:

1. **Submit a signed labor peace agreement** with their application, OR
2. **Submit a sworn statement** that the applicant has made a good-faith effort to enter into such an agreement and describe those efforts

**OCM Review:** The Office of Cannabis Management reviews labor peace compliance. Failure to maintain an agreement once required can result in license suspension or revocation.

### 12.3 Employee Count Threshold

- Threshold: **10 or more employees** (full-time and part-time combined)
- Counted at the time of license application and monitored throughout the license period
- If a licensee grows beyond 10 employees after initial licensure, the labor peace requirement is triggered

### 12.4 Selecting a Labor Organization

Common labor unions active in New York cannabis industry:
- **UFCW (United Food and Commercial Workers)** - most active in NY cannabis retail
- **Teamsters** - active in cannabis delivery and warehousing
- **RWDSU (Retail, Wholesale and Department Store Union)**

**Employer Options:**
- Proactively reach out to a labor organization to negotiate a labor peace agreement
- Respond to union outreach with good-faith negotiations
- Document all communications as evidence of good-faith effort for OCM

### 12.5 Penalties for Non-Compliance

- **License denial** if labor peace agreement not demonstrated at application
- **License suspension or revocation** for failure to maintain agreement during licensure period
- **Significant compliance risk** - OCM takes labor peace compliance seriously under MRTA

### 12.6 Compliance Checklist

- [ ] Determine if employee count meets or will meet 10-employee threshold
- [ ] Identify bona fide labor organization(s) active in NY cannabis industry
- [ ] Initiate and document good-faith outreach for labor peace agreement
- [ ] Obtain signed labor peace agreement before license application
- [ ] Include signed agreement with OCM license application
- [ ] Maintain agreement throughout license period
- [ ] Update OCM if labor peace agreement is terminated or renegotiated
