## 1. Control Area Definitions

### 1.1 What is a Control Area?

**Official Definition (IFC/IBC):**
> A control area is a space within a building where quantities of hazardous materials not exceeding the maximum allowable quantities per control area are stored, dispensed, used, or handled.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Control areas must be separated by fire barriers or horizontal assemblies
- Each control area has independent MAQ limits
- Multiple control areas can exist within a single building

### 1.2 Control Area Classification by Floor Level

Cannabis storage limits vary based on the **floor level relative to grade plane**:

| Floor Location | Control Area Level | % of Base MAQ Allowed | Fire Barrier Rating |
|----------------|-------------------|----------------------|---------------------|
| **Story 1 (Ground Level)** | **Level 1** | **100%** | 1 hour |
| Story 2 | Level 2 | 75% | 1 hour |
| Story 3 | Level 3 | 50% | 1 hour |
| Stories 4-6 | Level 4-6 | 12.5% | 2 hours |
| Stories 7-9 | Level 7-9 | 5% | 2 hours |
| Above Story 9 | Level 9+ | 5% | 2 hours |
| **Below Grade** | | | |
| 1st Basement | Basement Level 1 | 75% | 2 hours |
| 2nd Basement | Basement Level 2 | 50% | 2 hours |
| Below 2 Levels | Not Permitted | 0% | N/A |

**Source:** IBC Section 414.2, Table 414.2.2

### 1.3 Critical Compliance Note

**For NYC Retail Dispensaries:**
- Most operate on **Story 1 (Ground Level) = Level 1 Control Area**
- This allows **100% of the base MAQ**
- If located on Story 2, only **75% of base MAQ** is allowed
- Below-grade storage is **highly restricted** (75% max on first basement level)

## 2. Maximum Allowable Quantity (MAQ) Framework

### 2.1 MAQ Principles

**Core Concept (IFC Section 5003.1.1):**
> MAQ limits are material-specific and **independent by physical state** (solid, liquid, gas)

**Example:**
A control area can simultaneously contain:
- Solid hazardous materials (at full MAQ for solids)
- Liquid hazardous materials (at full MAQ for liquids)
- Gaseous hazardous materials (at full MAQ for gases)

**Source:** [ICC Code Corner: 2024 IFC Tables 5003.1.1](https://www.iccsafe.org/building-safety-journal/bsj-technical/code-corner-2024-international-fire-code-tables-5003-1-11-and-5003-1-12-maximum-allowable-quantities/)

### 2.2 MAQ Increases with Safety Features

**Automatic Sprinkler System:**
- Base MAQ × **2** (100% increase)
- Applies to most cannabis dispensaries

**Approved Storage Cabinets:**
- Base MAQ × **2** (for flammable liquids)

**Combination Example:**
- Base MAQ: 100 cubic feet
- With sprinklers: 200 cubic feet
- On Story 2 (75% allowed): 150 cubic feet actual limit

## 3. Cannabis Storage Limits - Chapter 40

### 3.1 NYC Fire Code Chapter 40 Status

**Important Note:**
- NYC Fire Code 2022 reorganized chapter numbering
- **Chapter 40 references distilled spirits and marijuana storage**
- Specific cannabis provisions found in **Section FC 4006**

**Verification Source:** NYC Fire Code 2022 Edition, Chapter 40, Section 4006

### 3.2 SECTION FC 4006 - CANNABIS STORAGE LIMITS

#### 3.2.1 Maximum Allowable Quantity (MAQ) by Building Type

**A. Sprinklered Buildings (Automatic Fire Suppression Installed):**

| Building Classification | MAQ Limit | Additional Requirements |
|------------------------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Mercantile (M) Occupancy** | **200 cubic feet**¹ | Standard retail dispensary classification |
| Group H (High-Hazard) | Unlimited² | Requires special H occupancy rating |
| Fully Sprinklered + M | 200 cubic feet | Most common dispensary scenario |

¹ *Base MAQ of 100 cubic feet × 2 (sprinkler multiplier) = 200 cubic feet*
² *Subject to OCM inventory limits and additional fire safety measures*

**B. Unsprinklered Buildings (No Automatic Fire Suppression):**

| Storage Type | Maximum Quantity | Storage Requirement |
|--------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| **Loose Combustible Fibers** | **100 cubic feet** | Metal/metal-lined bins with self-closing covers |
| Cannabis Flower (loose) | 100 cubic feet | Maximum for standard control area |
| Baled/Packaged Cannabis | 500 cubic feet | With 1-hour fire barrier enclosure |

**Source:** IFC Chapter 37, Section 3704

#### 3.2.2 Weight Equivalents (Estimated)

**Volume-to-Weight Conversion for Loose Cannabis Flower:**

| Volume (Cubic Feet) | Estimated Weight (Pounds)³ | Scenario |
|---------------------|---------------------------|----------|
| **100 cubic feet** | **15-30 lbs** | Unsprinklered, Level 1 Control Area |
| **200 cubic feet** | **30-60 lbs** | Sprinklered, Level 1 Control Area (100% MAQ) |
| 150 cubic feet | 22-45 lbs | Sprinklered, Level 2 Control Area (75% MAQ) |
| 100 cubic feet | 15-30 lbs | Sprinklered, Level 3 Control Area (50% MAQ) |

³ *Based on loose cannabis flower density of 0.15-0.30 lbs/ft³. Actual density varies by strain, moisture content, and packaging method.*

**⚠️ Important:** Fire code limits are **volume-based (cubic feet)**, not weight-based. The above conversions are for reference only.

#### 3.2.3 CO2 Extraction Systems

**CO2 Storage Permit Requirements:**

| CO2 Quantity | Permit Required | Additional Requirements |
|--------------|----------------|------------------------|
| ≤ 100 pounds | No permit needed | Standard safety protocols |
| **> 100 pounds** | **FDNY Permit Required** | Gas detection system, ventilation, emergency procedures |
| > 500 pounds | Special use permit | Enhanced safety systems |

**Source:** NYC Fire Code Chapter 53 (Compressed Gases)

## 4. Combustible Fibers Regulations - IFC Chapter 37

### 4.1 Cannabis as Combustible Fiber

**Classification:**
- Dried cannabis flower = **Loose Combustible Fiber**
- Fire Code Category: Same as cotton, hay, excelsior, hemp
- Hazard: High surface-area-to-volume ratio increases fire risk

### 4.2 Indoor Storage Requirements (IFC Section 3704)

#### Volume-Based Storage Tiers

**Tier 1: ≤ 100 Cubic Feet (No Special Enclosure)**
- **Storage Method:** Metal or metal-lined bin with self-closing cover
- **Fire Barrier:** Not required
- **Sprinkler:** Recommended but not mandatory
- **Example:** Small dispensary with limited inventory

**Tier 2: 101-500 Cubic Feet**
- **Storage Method:** Dedicated storage room
- **Fire Barrier:** 1-hour fire-resistance-rated walls
- **Opening Protection:** 3/4-hour rated door assemblies
- **Sprinkler:** Required if exceeding 200 cubic feet
- **Example:** Medium-sized dispensary with vault storage

**Tier 3: 501-1,000 Cubic Feet**
- **Storage Method:** Dedicated storage room
- **Fire Barrier:** 2-hour fire-resistance-rated walls
- **Opening Protection:** 1½-hour rated door assemblies
- **Sprinkler:** Mandatory
- **Example:** Large dispensary or distribution facility

**Tier 4: > 1,000 Cubic Feet**
- **Storage Method:** Dedicated storage room with enhanced protection
- **Fire Barrier:** 2-hour fire-resistance-rated walls
- **Opening Protection:** 1½-hour rated door assemblies
- **Sprinkler:** Mandatory with enhanced coverage
- **Additional:** May require Group H occupancy classification
- **Example:** Cultivation or processing facility (not typical retail)

**Source:** [IFC 2021 Chapter 37 - Combustible Fibers](https://up.codes/viewer/sioux-falls/ifc-2021/chapter/37/combustible-fibers)

### 4.3 Detached Storage (Alternative Option)

**Maximum for Detached Structures:**
- **2,500 cubic feet** maximum in detached building
- Must be located away from other structures
- Special fire separation distances required
- Not typical for urban NYC dispensaries

## 5. Practical Compliance Guide for NYC Dispensaries

### 5.1 Typical NYC Dispensary Scenario

**Most Common Configuration:**
- **Location:** Story 1 (Ground Level) = Level 1 Control Area
- **Building:** Sprinklered Mercantile (M) occupancy
- **MAQ Allowed:** 200 cubic feet (100 base × 2 sprinkler multiplier)
- **Estimated Weight:** 30-60 lbs of loose cannabis flower

### 5.2 Compliance Checklist

#### Step 1: Determine Your Control Area Level
- [ ] Identify floor level (Story 1, 2, 3, etc.)
- [ ] Calculate allowed MAQ percentage (100%, 75%, 50%, etc.)
- [ ] Verify no below-grade storage of large quantities

#### Step 2: Confirm Base MAQ
- [ ] Loose combustible fibers (cannabis flower): Base = **100 cubic feet**
- [ ] Apply sprinkler multiplier if applicable: 100 × 2 = **200 cubic feet**
- [ ] Apply control area level reduction if above Story 1

#### Step 3: Storage Configuration
| Your Inventory | Required Storage Method | Fire Code Requirement |
|----------------|------------------------|----------------------|
| ≤ 100 cu ft | Metal bins | No special room needed |
| 101-200 cu ft | Secure vault/room | 1-hour fire barrier |
| 201-500 cu ft | Dedicated storage room | 2-hour fire barrier + sprinklers |

#### Step 4: CO2 Systems (If Applicable)
- [ ] If using CO2 extraction: Check total CO2 storage
- [ ] If > 100 lbs CO2: Obtain FDNY permit
- [ ] Install gas detection and ventilation systems

#### Step 5: Documentation for FDNY Inspection
- [ ] Floor plan showing control area boundaries
- [ ] Fire barrier construction details (1-hour or 2-hour rating)
- [ ] Sprinkler system layout and approvals
- [ ] Inventory calculations (cubic feet, not weight)
- [ ] Storage method (bins, vault, dedicated room)

### 5.3 Common FDNY Inspection Issues

**❌ Violation 1: Exceeding MAQ Without Proper Enclosure**
- **Problem:** 300 cubic feet in open storage area
- **Solution:** Create 2-hour fire-rated storage room OR reduce inventory to ≤200 cu ft

**❌ Violation 2: Below-Grade Storage Without Justification**
- **Problem:** Basement storage with 100 cubic feet (would need basement-level MAQ reduction)
- **Solution:** Relocate to Story 1 OR reduce to 75 cubic feet (75% of base MAQ)

**❌ Violation 3: No Metal Bins for Small Quantities**
- **Problem:** Cardboard boxes for 50 cubic feet of loose flower
- **Solution:** Install metal or metal-lined bins with self-closing lids

**❌ Violation 4: CO2 Over 100 lbs Without Permit**
- **Problem:** 150 lbs of CO2 for extraction, no permit on file
- **Solution:** Apply for FDNY compressed gas storage permit immediately

### 5.4 FDNY Inspection Frequency

**Routine Inspections:**
- Initial certificate of occupancy inspection (before opening)
- Annual fire safety inspection
- Unannounced compliance checks

**Inspection Focus Areas:**
1. Verification of storage quantities (cubic feet measurement)
2. Fire barrier integrity (walls, doors, penetrations)
3. Sprinkler system functionality
4. Storage method compliance (metal bins, vault, etc.)
5. Permit status for CO2 or other compressed gases
