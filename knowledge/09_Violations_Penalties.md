## Violation Classification System

### 1.1 Minor Violations

**Definition:** Procedural violations that do not immediately threaten public safety or health and can be resolved through corrective measures.

**Typical Examples:**
- Delayed or incomplete record-keeping (non-critical records)
- Improper or outdated signage display
- Employee training documentation not updated timely
- Minor packaging label defects (but not affecting safety information)
- Minor business hours violations (a few minutes early or late)
- Minor cleanliness and hygiene standard non-compliance

**Penalty Range:**
- First violation: Written warning + Corrective Action Plan (CAP)
- Repeat violations: $500 - $2,000 fine
- Correction period: Usually 7-30 days

**Regulatory Basis:** 9 NYCRR Part 124-125

---

### 1.2 Serious Violations

**Definition:** Violations that pose substantial threat to public health, safety, or integrity of cannabis regulatory system.

**Typical Examples:**
- Failure to integrate or maintain BioTrack/Metrc seed-to-sale tracking system
- Inventory discrepancies exceeding 3% (not reported timely)
- Security camera system malfunction or missing recordings
- Sales to persons under age 21 (even if unintentional)
- Sale of untested or failed-testing products
- Unauthorized cross-tier conduct (e.g., retailer promoting proprietary product brand)
- Severely inaccurate or missing financial records
- Distance requirement violations (schools/religious facilities)

**Penalty Range:**
- First serious violation: $5,000 - $50,000 fine + mandatory compliance oversight
- Repeat serious violations: $50,000 - $100,000 fine + license suspension
- Correction period: Immediate - 60 days (depending on nature)
- May include: Enhanced compliance oversight (6-12 months)

**Case Reference:**
- July 2025, OCM fined one retailer and one processor $50,000 each for illegal cross-tier conduct and unauthorized brand promotion, plus 12 months enhanced compliance oversight.

**Regulatory Basis:** 9 NYCRR Parts 118-125, OCM Enforcement Guidelines

---

### 1.3 Criminal Violations

**Definition:** Intentional violations of law, fraud, diversion, or serious public safety endangerment that may result in criminal prosecution.

**Typical Examples:**
- Intentional sales to minors
- Large-scale product diversion to illegal market
- Forgery of test certificates or records
- Bribery of law enforcement or inspectors
- Manipulation of BioTrack/Metrc system data
- Major safety violations causing personal injury
- Money laundering or other financial crimes
- Unlicensed operation (without OCM license)

**Penalty Range:**
- Fines: $100,000 - $1,000,000+ (depending on case severity)
- Immediate license revocation
- Cannabis industry participation ban: 3-10 years or permanent
- Product recall (removal from all retail shelves)
- Criminal prosecution: Possible imprisonment

**Major Case:**
- **Omnium Canna Case:** OCM seeking license revocation, $1,000,000 fine, 3-year ban from New York cannabis industry, and recall of all Omnium products from retail shelves.

**Regulatory Basis:** MRTA (Marijuana Regulation & Taxation Act), Criminal Law, 9 NYCRR Parts 118-125

---

### 1.4 Unlicensed Operation Violations

**Special Category:** For businesses selling cannabis products without OCM license.

**Penalty Structure:**
- **Daily Continuing Violation:** Up to $10,000 fine
- **Revenue-Based Fines:**
  - 5 times illegal sales revenue, or
  - 3 times projected revenue of unsold illegal products
- **Violations After Cease and Desist Order:** Up to $20,000 daily
- **Refusal of Inspection:** $20,000 - $40,000 fine

**Enforcement Tools:**
- Padlock Order
- Emergency closure order
- Product seizure
- Criminal prosecution referral

**Regulatory Basis:** FY 2025 Budget Extension Law, OCM and Local Government Enforcement Powers

### 2.2 Cumulative Violation Escalation Mechanism

**Cumulative Violation Definition:** Multiple violations occurring within a 12-month period.

**Escalation Penalty Rules:**
- **1st Violation:** Standard penalty (warning or minimum fine)
- **2nd Violation (same type):** Fine increase 50-100%
- **3rd Violation (same type):** Fine increase 200% + license suspension risk
- **4th or More:** Automatic trigger of license revocation review

**Example Scenario:**
```
1st Inventory Reporting Delay: Written warning
2nd Inventory Reporting Delay: $1,000 fine
3rd Inventory Reporting Delay: $3,000 fine + 30-day license suspension review
4th Inventory Reporting Delay: License revocation hearing
```

**Mitigating Factors:**
- Proactive self-reporting of violations
- Rapid corrective action
- No violation history
- Cooperative investigation participation

**Aggravating Factors:**
- Intentional or reckless conduct
- Concealment or false information provision
- Violation history
- Refusal to correct or cooperate

---

### 2.3 Special Fine Calculation Methods

#### Revenue-Based Fines (Unlicensed Operations)
```
Method 1: Actual illegal sales revenue × 5
Method 2: Projected illegal product revenue × 3

OCM selects method producing higher fine
```

#### Product Recall Costs
```
Recall Cost = (Recalled units × Wholesale price) + Administrative costs + Destruction costs

Licensee bears all costs
```

## License Suspension and Revocation Process

### 3.1 License Suspension

**Trigger Conditions:**
- Serious violations (repeat or high-risk)
- Unpaid fines or taxes
- Failure to complete corrective action plan
- Uncooperative with inspection or investigation
- Financial difficulties preventing compliant operation

**Suspension Types:**

#### Emergency Suspension
- **Trigger Condition:** Imminent threat to public health/safety
- **Examples:** Sale of contaminated products, major security system failure, sales to minors
- **Procedure:** OCM can immediately suspend license without prior notice
- **Hearing:** Must hold hearing within 10 business days of suspension
- **Duration:** Until threat eliminated and inspection passed

#### Standard Suspension
- **Trigger Condition:** Serious violations without imminent danger
- **Procedure:** OCM issues notice of intent → Licensee has 15 days to respond → Hearing → Final decision
- **Duration:** Usually 30-180 days
- **Conditions:** May include restoration conditions (e.g., additional training, system upgrades, third-party audit)

**Impact During Suspension:**
- ❌ Cannot conduct any sales or transfers
- ❌ Cannot receive new products
- ✅ Must maintain security and inventory tracking systems
- ✅ Must continue paying rent and insurance
- ✅ Can prepare restoration plan

---

### 3.2 License Revocation

**Trigger Conditions:**
- Criminal violations
- Repeat serious violations (3 or more times)
- Fraud or intentional misrepresentation
- Major financial violations
- Sales to minors (intentional or repeat)
- Large-scale product diversion

**Revocation Process:**
1. **Investigation Phase:** OCM conducts comprehensive investigation (usually 30-90 days)
2. **Notice of Charges:** OCM issues formal charge letter (listing all violations)
3. **Response Period:** Licensee has 30 days to submit written response
4. **Hearing:** Formal administrative hearing (similar to court proceedings)
5. **Final Decision:** Hearing officer or OCM Board makes final decision
6. **Enforcement:** License formally revoked, must cease operations

**Consequences After Revocation:**
- 🚫 License immediately terminated
- 🚫 Banned from New York cannabis industry (usually 3-10 years or permanent)
- 💰 Must pay all outstanding fines
- 📦 Inventory must be transferred to other licensees or destroyed
- 📋 All records must be transferred to OCM
- ⚖️ May face additional civil or criminal litigation

**Case Study: Omnium Canna Revocation Case**
- **Charges:** Serious violation of compliance standards
- **OCM Seeking:**
  - License revocation
  - $1,000,000 fine
  - 3-year ban from New York cannabis industry
  - Recall products from all retail shelves
- **Timeline:** Investigation → Hearing → Expected final decision in 2026

---

### 3.3 Appeals Process

**Appealable Decisions:**
- License suspension
- License revocation
- Fine amount
- Corrective action requirements

**Appeal Steps:**

#### Step 1: Internal Review (OCM Reconsideration)
- **Deadline:** Must apply within 30 days of receiving final decision
- **Requirements:** Submit written appeal + supporting evidence
- **Review Body:** OCM Board or designated review officer
- **Timeline:** OCM usually decides within 60 days
- **Possible Outcomes:** Uphold, modify, or reverse original decision

#### Step 2: Judicial Review
- **Jurisdiction:** New York State Supreme Court
- **Deadline:** Must file within 60 days of OCM final decision
- **Review Standard:** "Arbitrary and capricious" or "lacking substantial evidence"
- **Procedure:** Review based on administrative record (no new fact-finding)
- **Possible Outcomes:** Uphold, reverse, or remand to OCM for reconsideration

**License Status During Appeal:**
- **Suspension:** Usually continues during appeal (unless court issues stay order)
- **Revocation:** Usually continues during appeal (unless temporary injunction obtained)
- **Fines:** May need to pay or provide security

**Appeal Success Factors:**
- Procedural errors (OCM failed to follow due process)
- Insufficient evidence to support violation finding
- Penalty disproportionate to violation severity
- Mitigating circumstances existed (OCM failed to adequately consider)

## Common Violation Case Studies

### 4.1 Age Verification Failure

**Case 1: Failed to Verify ID**
- **Facts:** Employee did not request ID from young-looking customer
- **Violation:** Failure to comply with age verification procedures (9 NYCRR Part 125)
- **Penalty:** First time - $10,000 fine + mandatory employee retraining
- **Prevention Measures:**
  - Implement "check everyone" policy (no exceptions)
  - Mandatory ID scanning at POS system
  - Regular mystery shopper testing

**Case 2: Accepted Expired ID**
- **Facts:** Employee accepted driver's license expired for 6 months
- **Violation:** Acceptance of invalid identification
- **Penalty:** First time - $5,000 fine + written warning
- **Prevention Measures:**
  - Train employees to check expiration dates
  - ID scanning software automatically detects expired IDs

**Case 3: Sales to Minor (Actual Sale Occurred)**
- **Facts:** Undercover minor successfully purchased cannabis products
- **Violation:** Sales to person under age 21 - **Zero tolerance**
- **Penalty:** $50,000 - $250,000 fine + license suspension or revocation
- **Legal Consequences:** Possible criminal charges
- **Prevention Measures:**
  - Mandatory ID scanner (refuses expired or fake IDs)
  - Dual verification system (two employees check)
  - Quarterly compliance audits

---

### 4.2 Inventory Tracking Violations

**Case 4: BioTrack/Metrc Integration Failure**
- **Facts:** Retailer failed to integrate BioTrack system within required timeframe
- **Violation:** Failure to maintain seed-to-sale tracking system
- **Penalty:** First time - $20,000 fine + 30-day correction period
- **Repeat Violation:** $50,000 fine + license suspension
- **Prevention Measures:**
  - Begin system integration 60 days in advance
  - Hire IT consultant or use certified POS vendor
  - Conduct thorough testing before going live

**Case 5: Inventory Discrepancy Exceeds 3%**
- **Facts:** Monthly audit found 5% inventory shortage (valued at $15,000)
- **Violation:** Failure to maintain accurate inventory records + failure to report within 24 hours
- **Penalty:** $30,000 fine + enhanced audit (weekly) for 90 days
- **Prevention Measures:**
  - Daily inventory reconciliation
  - Real-time POS integration (automatic deduction)
  - Weekly cycle counting
  - Security cameras covering all inventory areas

**Case 6: Falsified Inventory Records**
- **Facts:** Employee manually modified BioTrack data to hide shortages
- **Violation:** Data manipulation - **Criminal Violation**
- **Penalty:** $500,000 fine + immediate license revocation + criminal prosecution
- **Individual Consequences:** Involved employee may face criminal charges

---

### 4.3 Product Compliance Violations

**Case 7: Sale of Failed-Testing Products**
- **Facts:** Retailer sold batch of edibles with pesticide levels exceeding limits
- **Violation:** Sale of products failing testing
- **Penalty:** $100,000 fine + immediate recall + 60-day license suspension
- **Public Health Impact:** May lead to additional health department investigation
- **Prevention Measures:**
  - Verify test certificates in POS system
  - Refuse to receive products without valid COA (Certificate of Analysis)
  - Regular supplier audits

**Case 8: Non-Compliant Packaging/Labeling**
- **Facts:** Products lacking required warning labels
- **Violation:** Packaging violation (9 NYCRR Part 119)
- **Penalty:** First time - $5,000 fine + product removal until re-labeled
- **Repeat Violation:** $20,000 fine + possible supplier investigation
- **Prevention Measures:**
  - Implement product receiving checklist
  - Refuse non-compliant packaging
  - Establish quality agreements with suppliers

---

### 4.4 Security System Violations

**Case 9: Surveillance Video Gap**
- **Facts:** OCM inspector requested video from 45 days ago, but system only retained 30 days
- **Violation:** Failure to retain 60 days of surveillance video
- **Penalty:** First time - $10,000 fine + 15 days to upgrade system
- **Repeat Violation:** $30,000 fine + license suspension
- **Prevention Measures:**
  - Install large-capacity NVR/DVR (minimum 2TB for 60-day retention)
  - Monthly video retrieval testing
  - Set up automatic cloud archiving

**Case 10: Unreported Alarm System Failure**
- **Facts:** Alarm system malfunction for 5 days, failed to notify OCM or central monitoring station
- **Violation:** Failure to maintain functional security system + failure to report within 24 hours
- **Penalty:** $15,000 fine + immediate repair
- **Prevention Measures:**
  - Contract with licensed monitoring company (automatic reporting)
  - 30-day maximum inspection interval
  - Maintain 5-year inspection records

---

### 4.5 Financial and Tax Violations

**Case 11: Failure to File Quarterly Tax Return**
- **Facts:** Retailer missed NYS adult-use cannabis products tax quarterly deadline
- **Violation:** Tax violation
- **Penalty:** Tax Department fine + interest + possible OCM fine
- **Serious Situation:** Repeat non-filing may lead to license suspension
- **Prevention Measures:**
  - Set quarterly deadline reminders (20th of each quarter)
  - Hire cannabis-specialized accountant
  - File zero return even with zero sales

**Case 12: 280E Tax Audit Failure**
- **Facts:** IRS audit found improper deduction of operating expenses (non-COGS)
- **Violation:** Federal tax violation (not OCM, but affects license eligibility)
- **Penalty:** IRS fine + back taxes + interest
- **OCM Impact:** May trigger OCM financial review or license eligibility review
- **Prevention Measures:**
  - Dual-track accounting system (Federal COGS vs. NYS full deduction)
  - Detailed COGS documentation (invoices, timecards, batch records)
  - Hire accountant familiar with 280E

---

### 4.6 Cross-Tier and Branding Violations

**Case 13: Unauthorized Branding by Vertically Integrated Business**
- **Facts:** Retailer (also processor) promoted own proprietary product brand at retail store
- **Violation:** Prohibited cross-tier conduct
- **Actual Case:** July 2025, OCM fined retailer and processor $50,000 each
- **Additional Penalty:** 12 months enhanced compliance oversight
- **Prevention Measures:**
  - Clearly separate retail and processing business brands
  - Compliance review of all marketing materials
  - Avoid excessive promotion of own products at retail point

## Risk Management Recommendations

### 5.1 Preventive Compliance Strategies

**Establish Compliance Culture:**
- ✅ Emphasize importance of compliance from top management
- ✅ Regular (quarterly) all-staff compliance training
- ✅ Reward compliant behavior, not just punish violations
- ✅ Encourage employees to report potential issues (no retaliation policy)

**Implement Robust Systems:**
- ✅ Automate compliance checks (POS, inventory, ID verification)
- ✅ Redundant systems (backup power, dual security systems)
- ✅ Real-time monitoring and alerts (inventory discrepancies, system failures)

**Regular Audits:**
- ✅ Monthly internal compliance audits
- ✅ Quarterly third-party audits (recommended)
- ✅ Annual comprehensive compliance assessment

---

### 5.2 Incident Response Plan

**When Potential Violation Discovered:**
1. **Immediately Stop:** Suspend activity causing violation
2. **Assess:** Determine nature and severity of violation
3. **Document:** Detailed documentation of discovery process and facts
4. **Self-Report:** For serious violations, consider proactively reporting to OCM
5. **Correct:** Immediately implement corrective measures
6. **Root Cause Analysis:** Determine why it occurred and prevent recurrence

**Benefits of Self-Reporting:**
- Mitigate penalties (possible 25-50% reduction)
- Demonstrate integrity and willingness to cooperate
- Avoid "concealment" charges

**When Must Report (within 24 hours):**
- Inventory discrepancy > 3%
- Security system failure or breach
- Sales to minors (if discovered)
- Product safety issues or recall
- Any event possibly affecting public health or safety

---

### 5.3 License Protection Best Practices

**Financial Health:**
- 💰 Maintain at least 6 months operating reserve
- 💰 Reserve fine contingency fund ($50,000-$100,000)
- 💰 Pay all taxes and fees timely
- 💰 Maintain detailed financial records (7 years)

**Operational Excellence:**
- 🔐 Invest in high-quality security and tracking systems
- 🔐 Exceed compliance (not just meet minimum requirements)
- 🔐 Regular equipment upgrades and maintenance
- 🔐 Detailed SOP (Standard Operating Procedure) documentation

**Human Resources:**
- 👥 Thorough employee background checks
- 👥 Continuous training and certification
- 👥 Competitive compensation (reduce employee turnover and internal theft)
- 👥 Clear accountability and supervision chain

**Legal Protection:**
- ⚖️ Retain cannabis law specialist attorney
- ⚖️ Purchase comprehensive business liability insurance
- ⚖️ Regular legal compliance review
- ⚖️ Legal representation in any hearing or investigation

---

### 5.4 Insurance Considerations

**Recommended Insurance Types:**
1. **General Liability Insurance:** $1M-$2M coverage
2. **Product Liability Insurance:** Cover contaminated or mislabeled products
3. **Commercial Property Insurance:** Cover inventory, equipment, premises
4. **Cyber Liability Insurance:** Cover data breaches (customer ID information)
5. **Directors & Officers (D&O) Insurance:** Cover management decisions
6. **Employment Practices Liability Insurance:** Cover labor disputes
7. **Regulatory Compliance Insurance:** Cover fines and legal fees (if available)

**Insurance Considerations:**
- Many traditional insurance companies don't cover cannabis businesses
- Seek specialized cannabis insurance providers
- Expect premiums 2-5 times higher than traditional retail
- Carefully review exclusions (many policies don't cover intentional violations)

## Enforcement Trend Analysis (2025-2026)

   - FY 2025 budget expanded OCM and local government enforcement powers

   - Significantly increased fines ($10,000-$40,000 daily)
   - Expected: Continued intensified enforcement against illegal stores in 2026

2. **Cross-Tier Conduct Regulation:**
   - July 2025 Omnium case shows OCM focus on vertically integrated business conduct
   - Focus: Unauthorized brand promotion, proprietary product promotion
   - Expected: Continued scrutiny of vertically integrated businesses

3. **Seed-to-Sale Tracking Compliance:**
   - Transition from BioTrack to Metrc (early 2026)
   - Focus on ensuring all businesses complete transition
   - Expected: Increased enforcement of tracking system violations in Q1-Q2 2026

4. **Product Testing and Safety:**
   - Continued focus on contaminated products (pesticides, heavy metals, microorganisms)

   - Expected: Enhanced testing compliance inspections

5. **Youth Protection:**
   - Zero tolerance for sales to minors
   - Increased undercover minor purchase testing
   - Expected: Continued high-priority enforcement
