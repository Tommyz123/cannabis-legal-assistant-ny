## 12. Tax Compliance & Financial Obligations

**Primary Sources:** NYS Tax Law Article 20-C, IRC Section 280E

### 12.1 New York State Cannabis Taxes

| Tax Type | Rate | Tax Base | Taxpayer | Filing Frequency |
|----------|------|----------|----------|------------------|
| **Wholesale Excise Tax** | 9% | Distributor transfer price | Distributor | Quarterly |
| **Retail Excise Tax** | 13% | Retail customer price | Consumer (retailer collects) | Quarterly |
| **Vertically Integrated Additional Tax** | 9% of 75% | Retail sales × 75% | Vertically integrated retailers | Quarterly |

**Important Notes:**
- Adult-use cannabis products are **exempt from NYS sales tax**
- Only collect the excise taxes listed above
- Zero returns must be filed even if no sales occurred

### 12.3 Federal Tax Compliance (IRS Section 280E)

**⚠️ CRITICAL:** Cannabis remains a Schedule I controlled substance federally, triggering IRC § 280E restrictions.

| Aspect | Federal Rule | NYS Rule | Compliance Action |
|--------|--------------|----------|-------------------|
| **Business Expense Deductions** | NOT allowed (280E applies) | ALLOWED (NYS decoupled 2023) | ☐ Maintain dual accounting |
| **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)** | ALLOWED | ALLOWED | ☐ Maximize COGS categorization |
| **Record Retention** | 7 years recommended | 3 years minimum | ☐ Keep 7 years for safety |

**What CAN Be Deducted (Federal - COGS Only):**
- Product acquisition costs
- Direct production labor (if applicable)
- Inbound freight
- Packaging materials (directly touching product)
- Inventory handling labor

**What CANNOT Be Deducted (Federal):**
- Rent (non-production areas)
- Marketing and advertising
- Administrative salaries
- Legal and accounting fees
- Office supplies
- Insurance premiums
- Bank fees

**NYS Tax Advantage:**
- New York decoupled from 280E effective January 1, 2023
- All ordinary business expenses ARE deductible on **state tax returns**
- Must maintain separate calculations for federal vs. state filings

### 12.4 Tax Accounting Best Practices

**Action Items:**
- [ ] Hire cannabis-specialized CPA familiar with 280E
- [ ] Implement dual-track accounting system (COGS vs. Operating Expenses)
- [ ] Track employee time by activity (production vs. administrative)
- [ ] Maintain detailed COGS documentation (invoices, time cards, batch records)
- [ ] Use consistent inventory costing method (FIFO, Weighted Average, or Specific ID)
- [ ] Prepare for potential IRS audit (cannabis industry high-risk)
- [ ] Coordinate NYS quarterly filings with OCM BioTrack data
- [ ] Monitor DEA rescheduling developments (280E would not apply if moved to Schedule III)

### 12.5 Tax Calculation Example (Retail Dispensary)

**Scenario:** Vertically integrated microbusiness makes $100,000 in retail sales in Q1.

```
Retail Sales: $100,000

NYS Retail Excise Tax (13%):
  $100,000 × 13% = $13,000 (collected from customers)

NYS Distributor Tax (9% of 75%):
  $100,000 × 75% × 9% = $6,750 (paid by business)

Total NYS Cannabis Taxes: $19,750
Effective Tax Rate: 19.75%

Federal Income Tax:
  Gross Revenue: $100,000
  COGS (example): -$40,000
  Taxable Income: $60,000 (no other deductions allowed under 280E)
  Federal Tax (21% C-Corp): $12,600

NYS Income Tax:
  Gross Revenue: $100,000
  COGS: -$40,000
  Operating Expenses: -$25,000 (deductible in NYS!)
  Taxable Income: $35,000
  NYS Tax (6.5% example): $2,275
```

**Total Tax Burden in Example:**
- NYS Cannabis Excise Taxes: $19,750
- Federal Income Tax: $12,600
- NYS Income Tax: $2,275
- **Total: $34,625 (34.6% of revenue)**

This demonstrates the critical importance of maximizing COGS and proper tax planning.

## 13. Employee Rights & Labor Law Compliance

**Primary Sources:** NY Labor Law § 201-d, MRTA, NYS DOL Guidance

### 13.1 Protected Employee Cannabis Use

| Scenario | Time | Location | Equipment | Protected? | Employer Action Allowed? |
|----------|------|----------|-----------|------------|-------------------------|
| Weekend home use | Off-duty | Home | Personal | ✅ Yes | ❌ No |
| Lunch break in parking lot | Off-duty | Workplace | Personal | ❌ No | ✅ Yes - can prohibit |
| Remote work off-hours at home | Off-duty | Home | Personal | ✅ Yes | ❌ No |
| During work hours (remote) | Work hours | Home | Personal | ❌ No | ✅ Yes - can prohibit |
| Driving company vehicle | Any time | Any place | Company property | ❌ No | ✅ Yes - can prohibit |

**Core Principle:** Employees have the right to use cannabis **off-duty, off-premises, without employer equipment**. Employers cannot discriminate based on this legal activity.

### 13.2 Employer Rights & Restrictions

| Employer Action | Allowed? | Conditions | Law Reference |
|-----------------|----------|------------|---------------|
| **Prohibit use at workplace** | ✅ Yes | Always | § 201-d |
| **Prohibit use during work hours** | ✅ Yes | Even if remote | § 201-d |
| **Prohibit use of company property** | ✅ Yes | Vehicles, equipment, etc. | § 201-d |
| **Pre-employment drug testing** | ❌ No | Unless federal law requires | § 201-d |
| **Random drug testing** | ❌ No | Unless federal law requires | § 201-d |
| **Test based on cannabis odor alone** | ❌ No | Odor ≠ impairment | NYS DOL Guidance |
| **Test based on impairment symptoms** | ✅ Yes | Must be specific, articulable | NYS DOL Guidance |
| **Discipline for off-duty use** | ❌ No | Violates § 201-d | § 201-d |

### 13.3 Impairment Standard

**What Constitutes "Specific Articulable Symptoms of Impairment":**

✅ **Valid Indicators:**
- Operating machinery unsafely or recklessly
- Slowed speech or inability to communicate clearly
- Poor coordination or difficulty using tools
- Glazed eyes, lack of focus
- Significantly delayed reaction time
- Inability to complete normal job tasks

❌ **NOT Valid Indicators (Alone):**
- Cannabis odor on clothing or breath
- Positive drug test result (THC can remain for weeks)
- Admission of past use
- Possession of cannabis off-site

### 13.4 Drug Testing Policy Restrictions

**Prohibited Testing Scenarios:**
- ❌ Pre-employment testing for non-federal positions
- ❌ Random testing programs (unless DOT/federal requirement)
- ❌ Testing based solely on odor
- ❌ Testing as retaliation for asserting rights

**Permitted Testing Scenarios:**
- ✅ DOT-regulated positions (commercial drivers, etc.)
- ✅ Federal contractors with specific requirements
- ✅ Reasonable suspicion based on impairment symptoms
- ✅ Post-accident testing (if required by federal law)

**Action Items:**
- [ ] Review and update employee handbook cannabis policy
- [ ] Remove pre-employment cannabis testing (unless federal exception)
- [ ] Train managers on recognizing impairment vs. protected off-duty use
- [ ] Establish clear "specific articulable symptoms" documentation protocol
- [ ] Identify positions subject to federal testing requirements (if any)
- [ ] Post updated workplace cannabis policy
- [ ] Clarify remote work policies (employee home ≠ workplace)

### 13.5 Medical Cannabis Reasonable Accommodation

**Additional Considerations:**
- Employees with medical cannabis certifications may have additional protections under disability laws
- Employers may need to provide reasonable accommodations unless undue hardship
- Medical cannabis use still cannot occur during work hours or at the workplace
- Consult employment attorney for medical cannabis accommodation requests

### 13.6 Employee Rights Enforcement

**If Rights Violated:**
- Employee can file complaint with NYS Department of Labor
  - Phone: (888) 469-7365
  - Website: dol.ny.gov
- Employee can file civil lawsuit for damages
- Employee can file complaint with NYS Division of Human Rights

**Employer Liability Risks:**
- Wrongful termination claims
- Discrimination damages
- Attorney's fees and costs
- Reinstatement orders

**Action Items:**
- [ ] Ensure all managers understand § 201-d protections
- [ ] Document any disciplinary actions with specific job performance issues
- [ ] Never reference off-duty cannabis use in disciplinary records
- [ ] Maintain confidentiality of employee cannabis use information
- [ ] Consult employment attorney before any cannabis-related termination
