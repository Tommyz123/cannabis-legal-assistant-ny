# NYC Cannabis Business License Requirements
**New York City Department of Consumer and Worker Protection (DCWP) Compliance Guide**

## Document Overview

This document consolidates city-level regulatory requirements for New York City cannabis retail businesses, focusing on compliance responsibilities with NYC Department of Consumer and Worker Protection (DCWP) and other city-level agencies.

---

## 1. Regulatory Framework Overview

### 1.1 Regulatory Tier Structure

New York City cannabis businesses are subject to a **dual-tier regulatory system**:

| Regulatory Tier | Primary Agency | Regulatory Scope |
|-----------------|----------------|------------------|
| **State Level** | Office of Cannabis Management (OCM) | Primary license authorization, product standards, quality control |
| **City Level** | DCWP, DOB, FDNY, DOHMH | City compliance, building fire safety, health standards, labor protection |

**Important Note**: Based on latest information, **DCWP is not the primary licensing authority** for cannabis businesses. Cannabis retail licenses are issued by New York State OCM. DCWP plays a collaborative role in enforcement inspections.

### 1.2 DCWP's Regulatory Responsibilities

While DCWP does not issue cannabis licenses, it plays important roles in the following areas:

1. **Joint Enforcement Inspections**: Coordinated enforcement with OCM, Sheriff, NYPD
2. **Consumer Protection Enforcement**: Ensuring refund policy compliance
3. **Labor Protection Oversight**: Workplace safety and worker rights
4. **Illegal Store Crackdown**: Assisting in closing unlicensed operations

## 2. State-Level Primary Licensing Requirements (OCM)

### 2.1 License Types

New York State offers the following cannabis business licenses:

- **Retail Dispensary License**
- **Processor License**
- **Cultivator License**
- **Distributor License**
- **Microbusiness License**
- **Branding License**
- **Consumption Site License**

### 2.2 Application Process (4 Phases)

#### Phase 1: Primary License Application
- Business entity information
- Ownership structure disclosure
- Financial information disclosure
- Background check

#### Phase 2: Social and Economic Equity (SEE) Certification (Optional)
- SEE certified applicants receive **50% fee reduction**
- Priority application processing

#### Phase 3: True Parties of Interest (TPI) Disclosure
- Complete ownership chain
- All beneficial owner information
- Financial stakeholders

#### Phase 4: Location and Operations Section
- Site floor plan
- Facility renderings
- Operations plan
- Community impact plan
- Environmental plan

### 2.3 License Fee Structure

| License Type | Application Fee | License Fee | Total |
|--------------|----------------|-------------|-------|
| **Conditional Adult-Use Retail Dispensary** (CAURD) | $2,000 | - | $2,000 |
| **General Adult-Use Retail License** | $1,000 | $7,000 | $8,000 |
| **Limited Consumption Facility Add-on** | - | $3,000 | +$3,000 |

**SEE Certification Benefit**: All fees reduced 50%

### 2.4 License Renewal Requirements

**Renewal Time Window**: Submit 60-120 days before expiration

**Renewal Application Must Include**:
- Updated community impact plan
- Valid Labor Peace Agreement
- Employee demographics and wage information
- Applicable license fees

**Important Update**: All temporary CAURD and adult-use licenses have been extended to **December 31, 2026**.

## 3. Location Compliance Requirements

### 3.1 Site Selection Tool: LOCAL Map

OCM provides **LOCAL Map tool** for evaluating proposed retail locations:
- Check distance buffers from schools, playgrounds and other protected areas
- Verify location complies with municipal opt-out policies
- Available since January 17, 2025

### 3.2 Municipal Opt-Out Status

New York State has 1,520 municipalities:
- **750 municipalities** opted out of retail dispensaries
- **876 municipalities** opted out of consumption sites

**New York City Status**: Did not opt out, allowing cannabis retail and consumption sites

### 3.3 Distance Buffer Requirements

Retail dispensaries must maintain minimum distance from following facilities:
- Schools (K-12)
- Daycare centers
- Playgrounds
- Youth activity centers

*Note: Specific distance requirements are specified in OCM regulations; use LOCAL Map tool for verification*

## 4. City-Level Compliance Requirements

### 4.1 Department of Buildings (DOB) Requirements

Cannabis retail dispensaries must obtain the following building permits:

1. **Construction Permit** (if renovation)
2. **Certificate of Occupancy**
   - Use group compliance
   - Space layout approval
3. **Building/Fire Inspection** approval

### 4.2 Fire Department (FDNY) Requirements

1. **Fire Safety Inspection**
   - Fire extinguisher configuration
   - Exit signs and emergency lighting
   - Smoke detectors and alarm systems
2. **Hazardous Materials Storage** (if applicable)
3. **Fire Access Clearance**

### 4.3 Department of Health and Mental Hygiene (DOHMH)

While cannabis is not traditional food, may involve:
- Storage standards for cannabis edibles
- Facility hygiene requirements
- Employee health and safety training

### 4.4 DCWP Related Compliance Areas

#### Consumer Protection Compliance

**Refund Policy Disclosure Requirements** (NY State Law):
- Retailers must post refund policy in **conspicuous location**
  - On items
  - Store entrance
  - Near checkout counter

**Default 30-Day Return Policy**:
- If merchant does not post refund policy, consumers automatically have **30-day full refund or credit** rights
- Proof of purchase required
- Merchandise must not be used or damaged

**Cannabis Products Special Provisions**:
- Opened, used or partially consumed products **generally not returnable**
- Exception for factory defects or damaged products

#### Labor Protection Compliance

DCWP oversees the following labor protection matters:
1. **Workplace Safety**
2. **Anti-Discrimination Policies**
3. **Wage and Hour Compliance**
4. **Labor Peace Agreement** compliance

## 5. Employee Training and Labor Requirements

### 5.1 Responsible Workforce Training Program

**Regulatory Requirement**: All cannabis industry workers must complete training within **30 days** of hire

**Training Content**:
1. **Free Online Video Course**
   - Product safety
   - Workplace responsibility
2. **Licensee-Provided Training** (at least 2 hours)
   - Position-specific duties
   - Business operation procedures
3. **Implicit Bias and Cultural Competency Training** (at least 1 hour)

**Verification Requirement**: Employees must complete form certifying training completion

### 5.2 Labor Peace Agreement

**Mandatory Requirement**: Applicants must sign Labor Peace Agreement with legitimate labor organization

**Agreement Content**:
- Guarantee worker organizing rights
- Labor-management peaceful dispute resolution mechanism
- Union representative negotiation

### 5.3 Workplace Environment Standards

Cannabis businesses must maintain:
- **Alcohol-Free Workplace**
- **Drug-Free Workplace** (except legal consumption within limited consumption facilities)
- **Smoke-Free Workplace**

## 6. Inspection and Enforcement

### 6.1 Joint Enforcement Inspections

New York City cannabis business enforcement involves multi-agency collaboration:

| Agency | Inspection Focus |
|--------|------------------|
| **OCM** | License compliance, product standards, advertising restrictions |
| **DCWP** | Consumer protection, labor rights, refund policies |
| **Sheriff** | Illegal store closure, criminal investigation |
| **NYPD** | Public safety, minor sales |
| **FDNY** | Fire safety, hazardous materials storage |
| **DOB** | Building codes, occupancy compliance |

### 6.2 Operation Padlock

New York City conducts **Operation Padlock** to combat illegal cannabis stores:
- Unlicensed operation site seizures
- Multi-agency joint raids
- Criminal and civil penalties

**Legal Operators Should Know**:
- Keep license displayed in conspicuous location
- Cooperate with enforcement inspections
- Maintain all compliance documents current

## 7. Violations and Penalties

### 7.1 Common Violations

**License Violations**:
- Unlicensed operation
- Expired license not renewed
- License not displayed conspicuously

**Product Violations**:
- Selling untested products
- Non-compliant product labels
- Selling products from unlicensed sources

**Consumer Protection Violations**:
- Failure to post refund policy
- False or misleading advertising
- Price fraud

**Labor Violations**:
- Employees not completing training
- Lack of Labor Peace Agreement
- Workplace safety violations

### 7.2 Penalty Measures

**Administrative Penalties**:
- Warning letter
- Fines (amount depends on violation severity)
- License suspension
- License revocation

**Criminal Penalties** (Serious Violations):
- Unlicensed operation: May face criminal charges
- Sales to minors: Serious criminal penalties
- Large-scale violations: Federal investigation and prosecution

## 9. Compliance Best Practices

### 9.1 License Management

1. **Maintain License Validity**
   - Submit renewal 60-120 days before expiration
   - Track all expiration dates
   - Update any business changes timely

2. **Document Custody**
   - Retain all license application copies
   - File inspection reports and communications
   - Digitize backup important documents

3. **Change Notifications**
   - Ownership changes must be reported to OCM
   - Location changes require new application
   - Structure changes require TPI update

### 9.2 Operational Compliance

1. **Product Source Control**
   - Only purchase from licensed suppliers
   - Verify all product test certificates
   - Use Metrc system to track inventory

2. **Employee Management**
   - Complete training within 30 days for new employees
   - Retain training records at least 3 years
   - Regular implicit bias training updates

3. **Consumer Protection**
   - Clearly post refund policy
   - Establish complaint handling procedures
   - Maintain price transparency

### 9.3 Inspection Response

1. **Pre-Inspection Preparation**
   - Regular self-assessment of compliance status
   - Organize and update all documents
   - Train employees to respond to inspector questions

2. **Cooperation During Inspection**
   - Professional and courteous response
   - Provide requested documents
   - Document inspection content and findings

3. **Post-Inspection Follow-up**
   - Immediately correct identified issues
   - Retain remediation evidence
   - Request re-inspection if necessary

## 12. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1: Does DCWP issue cannabis licenses?**
A: No. Cannabis retail licenses are issued by New York State Office of Cannabis Management (OCM). DCWP plays collaborative role in enforcement inspections, focusing on consumer protection and labor rights.

**Q2: Do NYC cannabis businesses need separate city-level licenses?**
A: No specialized "cannabis license" needed, but must comply with building (DOB), fire (FDNY) and other city-level requirements, such as Certificate of Occupancy and fire inspections.

**Q3: How often do licenses need to be renewed?**
A: Licenses have specific expiration dates. Must submit renewal application 60-120 days before expiration. Currently all temporary licenses extended to December 31, 2026.

**Q4: What benefits does SEE certification provide?**
A: Social and Economic Equity (SEE) certification provides:
- Application and license fees **50% reduction**
- Priority application processing
- Technical assistance resources

**Q5: How to report illegal cannabis stores?**
A: Can report through:
- Dial 311
- Online submission: 311.nyc.gov
- Email: [email protected]
- OCM incident report form: https://cannabis.ny.gov/report-an-incident

**Q6: Can cannabis products be returned?**
A: Depends on merchant policy and product status:
- Merchant must clearly post refund policy
- Opened or used products generally not returnable
- Factory defects or damaged products may be exceptions
- If no posted policy, default 30-day return right applies (but must be unused)

**Q7: Is employee training mandatory?**
A: Yes. All cannabis industry employees must complete OCM's Responsible Workforce Training Program within **30 days** of hire, including online courses and employer-provided training.

**Q8: What is a Labor Peace Agreement?**
A: Labor Peace Agreement is an agreement with legitimate labor organization guaranteeing worker organizing rights and commitment to peaceful labor-management dispute resolution. This is mandatory requirement for license applications.

**Q9: How frequent are inspections?**
A: No fixed schedule. OCM and city agencies can conduct:
- Routine compliance inspections
- Complaint-triggered inspections
- Random spot checks
- Pre-renewal inspections

**Q10: How many municipalities in New York City opted out of cannabis retail?**
A: Of New York State's 1,520 municipalities, 750 opted out of retail dispensaries, 876 opted out of consumption sites. **New York City did not opt out**, allowing cannabis retail and consumption sites.
