# New York State Cannabis Tax Guidance

## Document Overview
This document provides state-level and federal-level tax compliance guidance for New York cannabis businesses, including NYS Article 20-C excise tax regulations and IRS 280E expense deduction limitations.

**Last Updated:** February 8, 2026
**Primary Regulations:** NYS Article 20-C, IRC Section 280E

---

## Part I: New York State Tax Guidance (Article 20-C)

### 1.1 Legal Basis
- **Regulation Name:** Tax on Adult-use Cannabis Products
- **Regulation Section:** NYS Tax Law Article 20-C
- **Governing Agency:** NYS Department of Taxation and Finance
- **Official Website:** https://www.tax.ny.gov/bus/auc/

### 1.2 Excise Tax Rate Structure

#### Wholesale Excise Tax
- **Tax Rate:** 9%
- **Tax Base:** Distributor's transfer price to retailer
- **Taxpayer:** Distributor
- **Collection Point:** Wholesale to retail transfer

#### Retail Excise Tax
- **Tax Rate:** 13%
- **Tax Base:** Retail sales price (Customer price)
- **Taxpayer:** Consumer
- **Collection Agent:** Retailer

**Retail Excise Tax Distribution:**
- State Portion: 9%
- Local Government Portion: 4% (allocated to municipality where retailer is located)

#### Additional Local Tax Trusts
- **County/Major City Trust:** 1% (applicable to counties or cities with population exceeding 1 million)
- **Municipal Trust:** 3% (applicable to municipality of location)

### 1.3 Special Case: Vertically Integrated Operators

**Applicable To:**
- Registered Organizations
- Microbusinesses

**Tax Treatment:**
If vertically integrated operators sell adult-use cannabis products directly to retail consumers, they must:
- Pay retail excise tax (13%)
- Also pay distributor tax (9%), with tax base calculated as **75%** of retail sales amount

**Calculation Example:**
```
Retail Sales Amount: $1,000
Retail Excise Tax: $1,000 × 13% = $130
Distributor Tax: $1,000 × 75% × 9% = $67.50
Total Tax Burden: $197.50 (effective tax rate 19.75%)
```

### 1.4 Sales Tax Exemption

**Important Note:** Adult-use cannabis products are **exempt from New York State sales tax** (Sales Tax Exempt)

This means:
- Retailers do not need to collect regular sales tax
- Only need to collect excise tax as specified in Article 20-C
- Simplifies tax calculation process

### 1.5 Filing and Payment Requirements

#### Filing Cycle
- **Frequency:** Quarterly filing
- **Quarterly Deadlines:**
  - February Quarter (Dec-Feb): March 20
  - May Quarter (Mar-May): June 20
  - August Quarter (Jun-Aug): September 20
  - November Quarter (Sep-Nov): December 20

#### Filing Method
- **Must file online**
- Use NYS Tax Department's **Adult-Use Cannabis Products Tax Web File application**
- Must submit **zero return** even if no sales during quarter

#### Record Retention Requirements
**Retention Period:** 3 years

**Start Date:** Whichever is later:
- Filing deadline date, or
- Date actually filed

**Required Records:**
- All tax records and transaction records
- Any additional records required by Office of Cannabis Management
- Sales invoices and receipts
- Purchase vouchers
- Inventory records
- Bank statements

### 1.6 Important Differences Between NYS and Federal Tax Law

**NYS Decoupling from IRC Section 280E:**
- **Effective Date:** Starting January 1, 2023
- **Important Impact:** New York State allows cannabis businesses to deduct ordinary business expenses in **state tax returns**
- **Practical Significance:** Although federal returns are subject to 280E restrictions, NYS returns can deduct operating expenses normally

**Deductible Expenses (State Tax Only):**
- Wages and salaries
- Rent
- Utilities
- Marketing and advertising expenses
- Professional services fees
- Insurance premiums
- Office supplies

## Part II: Federal Tax Guidance (IRS Section 280E)

### 2.1 Legal Basis

#### Regulation Information
- **Regulation Section:** Internal Revenue Code Section 280E
- **Enactment Year:** 1982
- **Legislative Purpose:** Prohibit businesses trafficking in controlled substances from deducting business expenses

#### Statute Text
"No deduction or credit shall be allowed for any amount paid or incurred during the taxable year in carrying on any trade or business if such trade or business (or the activities which comprise such trade or business) consists of trafficking in controlled substances (within the meaning of schedule I and II of the Controlled Substances Act) which is prohibited by Federal law or the law of any State in which such trade or business is conducted."

#### Applicability Rationale
- Cannabis remains classified as a **Schedule I controlled substance** by DEA
- Despite state law legalization, federal law still considers it illegal
- Therefore 280E automatically applies to all cannabis businesses

### 2.2 Insufficient IRS Guidance

**Lack of Official Guidance:**
- IRS provides **very little** guidance on 280E application to cannabis industry
- 2020 TIGTA report indicated: Low 280E compliance rate among cannabis businesses
- TIGTA recommendation: IRS needs to provide more industry-specific guidance

**Challenges Faced by Businesses:**
- Lack of clear rules leads to inconsistent interpretation
- High audit risk
- Need to rely on professional tax advisors

### 2.3 Deductible Expense: Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)

#### The Only Exception
While 280E prohibits deduction of business expenses, **Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)** can be deducted.

#### Legal Basis for COGS
- COGS is not a "deduction" but a necessary component of calculating gross profit
- Based on IRC Section 61 and 263A provisions
- Part of calculating "gross income," not subject to 280E limitation

#### Costs That Can Be Included in COGS

**Cultivators:**
- Seed or clone costs
- Growing medium (soil, hydroponic systems)
- Fertilizer and nutrient solutions
- **Direct cultivation labor:** Wages for growing, trimming, harvesting personnel
- Depreciation of cultivation facilities (per Section 263A rules)
- Utilities directly used for cultivation (proportionally allocated)

**Processors:**
- Raw materials (cannabis flower, leaves)
- Packaging materials (directly contacting product)
- **Direct processing labor:** Wages for extraction, formulation, packaging personnel
- Processing equipment depreciation
- Laboratory testing fees directly related to production

**Retailers/Dispensaries:**
- Cost of purchasing cannabis products
- Inbound freight
- **Labor directly related to inventory handling** (receiving, storage, labeling)

#### Key Principle: Directness Test
Expenses must be "directly and inseparably related to production or procurement of goods" to be included in COGS.

### 2.4 Non-Deductible Expenses

The following expenses can **never be deducted** (unless included in COGS):

#### Sales and Marketing Expenses
- Advertising and marketing costs
- Social media promotion
- Brand building activities
- Customer loyalty programs

#### Administrative Expenses
- Front desk reception wages
- Accounting and bookkeeping fees (external)
- Legal and compliance consulting fees
- Office supplies
- IT and software subscriptions

#### Operating Expenses
- Rent (non-production areas)
- Utilities (non-production areas)
- Insurance premiums
- Banking service fees
- Credit card processing fees

#### Employee-Related Expenses
- Management salaries (non-production related)
- Employee benefits and health insurance
- Training expenses
- Travel and conference expenses

#### Other Expenses
- Meals and entertainment
- Charitable donations
- License and franchise fees (not directly attributed to production costs)
- Professional association dues
- Website design and maintenance

### 2.5 Best Practices for 280E Compliance

#### Separate Accounting Systems
**Establish Dual-Track Accounting System:**
1. **COGS Account:** All direct production costs
2. **Operating Expense Account:** All indirect expenses (non-deductible under 280E)

**Importance:** Ability to clearly demonstrate expense classification logic during audit

#### Detailed Labor Time Tracking
**Issue:** Labor costs are typically the largest and most contentious item in COGS

**Solution:**
- Implement time clock system
- Distinguish production time from non-production time
- Classify by activity type (cultivation, processing, sales, administrative)
- Generate monthly time allocation reports

**Example:**
```
Employee: Zhang San
Total Hours: 160 hours/month
Cultivation Activity: 100 hours (62.5%) → Can be included in COGS
Administrative Activity: 40 hours (25%) → Not deductible
Sales Activity: 20 hours (12.5%) → Not deductible
```

#### Inventory Costing Method Selection
**Available Methods:**
- Specific Identification
- First-In, First-Out (FIFO)
- Weighted Average

**Recommendation:** Must maintain consistency after selection; changes require IRS application

#### Section 263A Uniform Capitalization Rules
**Applicable To:** Businesses with average annual gross receipts exceeding $25M

**Requirement:** Allocate certain indirect costs to inventory costs

**Indirect Costs That May Need Capitalization:**
- Production supervisor salaries
- Quality control costs
- Production facility maintenance
- Depreciation (proportional)

### 2.6 IRS Audit Triggers and Risk Points

#### High-Risk Areas

**1. Income Reporting Discrepancies**
- **Trigger:** Reported income doesn't match income reported by third-party payment processors
- **High-Risk Reason:** Large cash transactions make it easy to underreport income
- **Compliance Recommendation:**
  - Implement strict cash management system
  - Daily reconciliation
  - POS system integration with accounting system

**2. Inaccurate COGS Calculation**
- **Trigger:** COGS as percentage of revenue is abnormally high or low
- **Common Errors:**
  - Incorrectly including indirect expenses in COGS
  - Labor cost allocation without documentation
  - Inventory adjustments without supporting documentation
- **Compliance Recommendation:** Detailed cost allocation method documentation

**3. Expense Classification Errors**
- **Trigger:** Still listing operating expense deductions on Schedule C or Form 1120
- **Error Example:** Entering numbers in "Rent" line (should be 0)
- **Compliance Recommendation:** Ensure tax forms only show COGS, other expense lines should be blank or marked "280E Not Applicable"

**4. Multi-State Operation Complexity**
- **Trigger:** Multi-state operations with unclear cost allocation
- **Compliance Recommendation:** Separate accounting for each state/entity

#### IRS Audit Focus

Based on IRS audit experience, auditors primarily focus on:

1. **COGS Supporting Documentation:**
   - Vendor invoices
   - Payroll records and timesheets
   - Inventory in/out records
   - Cost allocation method documentation

2. **Record Completeness:**
   - All transactions have vouchers
   - Cash receipts have daily journals
   - Bank deposits consistent with reported income

3. **Consistency:**
   - Accounting methods consistent across years
   - Income consistent between state and federal returns (only expense deductions differ)

### 2.7 Record Retention Requirements

#### Federal Minimum Requirements
- **Retention Period:** At least **7 years** (IRS can look back 3-6 years, but cannabis industry recommended 7 years)
- **Retention Format:** Paper or electronic acceptable, but must be readily retrievable

#### Document Categories That Must Be Retained

**1. Income Documentation:**
- All sales receipts and invoices
- POS system reports (daily, monthly, annual)
- Bank deposit records
- Credit card batch reports
- Metrc system sales tracking records

**2. COGS Documentation:**
- Vendor invoices and purchase orders
- Inventory receiving and issue documents
- Production logs and batch records
- Payroll records and timecards
- Depreciation schedules

**3. Banking and Financial Documents:**
- Bank statements
- Check copies or electronic payment confirmations
- Loan documents and repayment records

**4. Compliance Documents:**
- OCM licenses and renewal records
- Laboratory test results
- Metrc inventory adjustment reports
- Employee training records

#### Organization Recommendations

**File Folder Structure by Category:**
```

│   │   ├── Q2 Sales Report.pdf
```

### 2.8 Major Development in 2026: Cannabis Rescheduling

#### Policy Change
- **Date:** December 18, 2025
- **Content:** New executive order issued prioritizing cannabis rescheduling from Schedule I to **Schedule III**

#### Impact on 280E

**If cannabis is rescheduled to Schedule III:**
- IRC Section 280E will **no longer apply** to cannabis businesses
- Reason: 280E only applies to Schedule I and II controlled substances

**Potential Impact on Businesses:**
- Can deduct all ordinary and necessary business expenses like other legal businesses
- Tax burden will significantly decrease (for many businesses, effective tax rate may drop 20-40%)
- Tax compliance complexity greatly simplified

#### Considerations
- **Current Status:** As of February 2026, cannabis remains Schedule I, 280E still applies
- **Timing Uncertain:** Specific timeline for rescheduling not yet clear
- **Business Recommendation:**
  - Continue complying with 280E rules
  - Closely monitor DEA and IRS announcements
  - Prepare to adjust accounting systems when policy changes

## Part III: Comprehensive Tax Compliance Recommendations

### 3.1 Entity Structure Considerations

#### Entity Type Selection
**Common Structures:**
- **C Corporation:** Subject to double taxation, but faces 21% flat federal tax rate
- **S Corporation:** Pass-through entity, but still subject to 280E limitation
- **LLC (treated as partnership or sole proprietorship):** Most common, high flexibility

**Considerations Under 280E:**
- Entity type cannot avoid 280E
- But can optimize COGS allocation through structural design

### 3.2 Vertical Integration Advantages

**Tax Perspective Benefits:**
1. **COGS Maximization:** More activities (cultivation, processing, retail) can be included in COGS
2. **Clearer Cost Tracking:** Internal transfer pricing controllable
3. **Avoid Multiple Taxation:** NYS has special rules for vertical integration

**Considerations:**
- Vertical integration in NYS requires paying distributor tax on 75% base
- Internal transfer pricing must comply with fair market value principles

### 3.3 Tax Technical Team Configuration

**Recommended Professional Support:**
1. **Cannabis-Specialized CPA:** Familiar with 280E and state tax differences
2. **Tax Attorney:** Handle complex structures and audit defense
3. **Bookkeeper:** Daily accurate recording, specialized COGS classification

**Cost-Benefit:** Professional tax planning typically saves multiples of service fees in taxes

### 3.4 Annual Tax Planning Timeline

#### Q1 (January-March)
- Prior year tax return preparation
- COGS method review
- Discuss 280E strategy with CPA

#### Q2 (April-June)
- File federal and state tax returns
- Review Q1 financials, adjust cost allocation
- Update internal controls

#### Q3 (July-September)
- Mid-year tax health check
- Estimate annual tax burden
- Adjust withholding/estimated tax if necessary

#### Q4 (October-December)
- Year-end tax planning
- Inventory count
- Confirm depreciation and capital expenditures
- Monitor 280E rescheduling progress

### 3.5 State and Federal Tax Coordination

**Return Difference Management:**
- Federal Forms: Only COGS deductible
- New York State Forms: All ordinary business expenses deductible

**Practical Operation:**
1. Prepare federal tax return (subject to 280E limitation)
2. Prepare New York State form, starting from federal taxable income with adjustments
3. Add 280E adjustment items on state form, subtracting expenses not deductible federally

**Example Adjustment:**
```
Federal Taxable Income: $500,000 (280E applied)
NYS Adjustment Items:
  - Marketing Expenses: -$50,000
  - Administrative Wages: -$100,000
  - Rent (Non-Production): -$30,000
NYS Taxable Income: $320,000
```
