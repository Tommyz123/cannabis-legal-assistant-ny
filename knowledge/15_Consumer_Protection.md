## 1. Product Recall System

### 1.1 Recall Regulatory Framework

New York State cannabis product recalls are uniformly managed by **Office of Cannabis Management (OCM)**.

**Legal Basis**:
- Cannabis Law
- OCM Quality Assurance Regulations
- Product Testing Standards (Part 129)

**Recall Trigger Conditions**:
- Products fail laboratory testing (pesticides, heavy metals, microorganisms)
- Non-compliant or misleading product labeling
- Products from unlicensed sources
- Falsified or invalid test results
- Other public health and safety risks

### 1.2 Recall Types and Classifications

| Recall Level | Definition | Response Time | Examples |
|--------------|------------|---------------|----------|
| **Class I** | Serious health hazard or death risk | Immediate (within 24 hours) | Pathogenic microorganisms, highly toxic contaminants |
| **Class II** | May cause temporary health problems | 48-72 hours | Pesticide exceedances, heavy metal exceedances |
| **Class III** | Unlikely to cause health problems | 5-7 business days | Labeling errors, THC content deviations |

### 1.4 Participant Responsibilities in Recalls

#### OCM Responsibilities

1. **Investigation and Assessment**
   - Receive test failure reports
   - Assess public health risk
   - Determine recall scope and level

2. **Issue Recall Order**
   - Publish recall announcement
   - List affected products and batches
   - Specify recall timeline

3. **Supervise Implementation**
   - Track recall progress
   - Verify product recovery and destruction
   - Evaluate implementation effectiveness

4. **Follow-up Enforcement**
   - Investigate violation causes
   - Propose administrative or criminal charges
   - Implement license sanctions

#### Producer/Processor Responsibilities

1. **Immediate Response** (After receiving recall order)
   - Stop distribution of affected products
   - Notify all distributors and retailers
   - Quarantine affected products in inventory

2. **Documentation**
   - Complete distribution records for affected batches
   - Product destination tracking (Metrc system)
   - Notification proof (emails, phone records)

3. **Product Recovery**
   - Arrange retailer returns
   - Centralized recovery of affected products
   - Destruction under OCM supervision

4. **Corrective Measures**
   - Root cause analysis report
   - Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPA)
   - Quality management system improvements

#### Retailer Responsibilities

1. **Immediate Execution** (After receiving recall notification)
   - Immediately remove products from shelves
   - Post recall notice in conspicuous location
   - Update inventory system to mark recalled products

2. **Consumer Notification** (If purchase records available)
   - Notify known buyers via phone/email/text
   - Provide recall details and return instructions
   - Document notification attempts

3. **Process Returns**
   - Accept consumer returns of recalled products
   - Provide full refund (even if opened)
   - Record return quantities and consumer information

4. **Cooperate with Investigation**
   - Provide sales records
   - Assist in determining product destination
   - Report any related consumer complaints

#### Consumer Rights and Responsibilities

**Consumer Rights**:
- Right to receive recall information
- Right to full refund (including opened products)
- Right to report adverse reactions
- Right to return without receipt (for recalled products)

**Consumer Responsibilities**:
- Stop using recalled products
- Return products to purchase location
- Report any adverse health reactions
- Retain product packaging for traceability

### 1.5 Recall Inquiry and Notification System

**Query Recall Information**:
1. **OCM Recalls Page**
   - URL: https://cannabis.ny.gov/recalls
   - Real-time updates of all active recalls
   - Search by date, product type, brand

2. **In-Store Notices**
   - Recall notices must be posted in conspicuous location
   - Include product images, batch numbers, health risk descriptions

3. **Consumer Registration System**
   - Some retailers offer purchase registration
   - Automatic recall notifications

**Automatic Notification Channels**:
- OCM official website banner announcements
- Email subscription (OCM newsletter)
- Social media (OCM Twitter, Facebook)
- Mainstream media press releases

## 3. Return and Refund Policies

### 3.1 New York State Refund Law Framework

**Legal Basis**:
- General Business Law § 218-A: Refund policy disclosure
- NYC Administrative Code § 5-37: NYC refund policy requirements

### 3.2 Mandatory Refund Policy Disclosure

**Legal Requirement**: All retailers must post refund policy in **conspicuous location**

**Posting Locations (at least one)**:
- Items themselves (on price tags)
- Store entrance
- At or near checkout counter

**Disclosure Content Requirements**:
- Return time window
- Refund form (cash, credit, exchange)
- Required proof (receipt, original packaging, etc.)
- Types of non-returnable merchandise
- Any return fees

### 3.3 Default 30-Day Return Policy

**Trigger Condition**: Merchant fails to post refund policy

**Automatic Rights**:
- Can return within **30 days** of purchase
- Consumer chooses **full refund or credit**
- Must provide proof of purchase
- Merchandise must be unused and undamaged

### 3.4 Special Provisions for Cannabis Products

#### Returnable Situations

1. **Product Defects**
   - Factory seal broken
   - Packaging damage causing product quality issues
   - Product deterioration or mold (before expiration)

2. **Labeling Errors**
   - THC/CBD content labeling errors
   - Missing or incorrect allergen information
   - Batch number doesn't match actual

3. **Unopened Products** (per merchant policy)
   - Complete original factory seal
   - Within expiration date
   - With proof of purchase

4. **Recalled Products**
   - **Must accept returns**, even if opened
   - Full refund, no receipt required
   - Cannot charge any fees

#### Generally Non-Returnable Situations

1. **Opened Products** (non-recall)
   - Opened flower products
   - Used vape pens
   - Opened edibles

2. **Consumed Products**
   - Partially used products
   - Empty containers

3. **Hygiene and Safety Reasons**
   - Prevent product contamination
   - Avoid re-entering supply chain
   - Protect other consumer safety

**Exception**: In case of adverse health reactions, retailers may accept used products for investigation

### 3.5 Refund Form Requirements

**Legal Provisions**: Refund form must be clearly disclosed before purchase

**Acceptable Refund Forms**:
1. **Cash Refund**
   - Full price refund
   - Applicable to cash purchases

2. **Original Payment Method Refund**
   - Credit/debit card refund
   - Electronic payment refund (3-5 business days)

3. **Store Credit**
   - Usable for future purchases
   - Usually no expiration date (per merchant policy)

4. **Exchange**
   - Equivalent value replacement product
   - Requires consumer consent

**Prohibited Conduct**:
- Only offering store credit without disclosure
- Charging "restocking fee" or "return handling fee" (unless pre-disclosed)
- Refusing refunds for recalled products

### 3.6 Return Process Standard Procedure

**Consumer Preparation**:
- [ ] Check if product meets return conditions
- [ ] Prepare proof of purchase (receipt, order confirmation)
- [ ] Retain original packaging and labels
- [ ] Keep unopened products sealed

### 3.7 Retailer Return Policy Best Practices

#### Policy Design

**Balance Consumer Rights and Business Risk**:

**Recommended Policy Framework**:
```
Sample Standard Return Policy:

1. Unopened Products
   - Time: Within 30 days of purchase
   - Condition: Original packaging seal intact, within expiration
   - Refund: Full refund or exchange, consumer's choice

2. Opened Products
   - Principle: No returns accepted
   - Exceptions: Product defects, labeling errors, adverse health reactions

3. Recalled Products
   - Accept all returns regardless of opened status
   - Full refund, no receipt required

4. Refund Form
   - Original payment method refund
   - Or store credit (consumer's choice)

5. Required Proof
   - Purchase receipt or order confirmation
   - Valid identification
```

#### Policy Communication

**Multi-Channel Disclosure**:
1. **In-Store Signage**
   - Conspicuous location at entrance
   - Behind checkout counter wall
   - Price tag notation

2. **Receipt Printing**
   - Return policy summary
   - Customer service contact information

3. **Website/App**
   - Dedicated return policy page
   - Checkout page link

4. **Employee Training**
   - Ability to clearly explain policy
   - Standard procedure for processing returns

#### Documentation and Tracking

**Return Data Analysis**:
- Track return reasons
- Identify product quality trends
- Optimize inventory management
- Improve supplier selection

**Metrc System Recording**:
- Returned products must be recorded in Metrc
- Mark as "return pending inspection"
- Track final disposition (destruction, return to supplier)

## 4. Case Studies

### Case 1: IndoGro LLC Large-Scale Product Recall (June 2025)

#### Background

**Time**: Initial recall June 11, 2025, expanded recall June 25

**Products Involved**:
- Initial recall: 9 product batches
- Expanded recall: Additional 28 product batches
- Brand names: Indi, Grow by Indi, Our Creation, Grow with Us

**Issue Discovery**:
1. Test results submitted by OCM-licensed laboratory found to be invalid
2. Re-testing found 9 batches contained **impermissible pesticide levels**
3. Further investigation found 28 batches used **materials from unlicensed entities**

#### Recall Implementation

**OCM Actions**:
- Issued public recall order
- Required all retailers to immediately remove from shelves
- Established consumer hotline
- Posted recall details on official website

**Health Risk Assessment**:
- Pesticide exceedances may cause health issues
- Unlicensed sources violate public health standards
- Unable to guarantee product safety

**Consumer Notification**:
- OCM website announcement
- Media press release
- In-store notices posted by retailers

#### Consumer Impact

**Reported Adverse Reactions**: As of recall announcement, OCM **received no** consumer adverse reaction reports

**Return Rights**:
- Consumers can contact retail pharmacy where purchased
- Can return recalled products **even if opened**
- Full refund

#### Follow-up Enforcement

**Root Cause Analysis**:
- Laboratory test result falsification
- Insufficient supply chain oversight
- Use of unlicensed materials serious violation

**Expected Penalties**:
- Administrative penalties and fines
- Possible license suspension or revocation
- Potential criminal charges

#### Lessons and Insights

**For Producers**:
1. Strictly verify laboratory qualifications
2. Review all test results for authenticity
3. Ensure raw materials from licensed suppliers
4. Establish complete supply chain traceability

**For Retailers**:
1. Verify supplier license validity
2. Respond quickly to recall orders
3. Maintain consumer purchase records for notification
4. Establish recall emergency procedures

**For Consumers**:
1. Check OCM recalls page
2. Retain purchase receipts and product packaging
3. Report any adverse reactions
4. Stop using recalled products

### Case 2: Omnium Health "License Renting" Scandal (October 2025)

#### Background

**Time**: October 20, 2025

**Parties Involved**: Omnium Health Inc. (operating as Omnium Canna)

**Violations**:
- Allowed **unlicensed operators** to use its licensed processing and distribution facilities
- "Reverse licensing" or "rent-a-license" scheme
- Enabled **out-of-state brands** to manufacture and sell products in New York without authorization

**Recall Scope**:
- Statewide recall
- Estimated retail value exceeding **$30 million**

#### OCM Enforcement Action

**Charges Filed**:
- Multiple Cannabis Law violations
- License abuse
- Fraudulent business practices
- Public health and safety endangerment

**Recall Order**:
- All Omnium-related products
- Immediate removal from all retail stores
- Consumers can receive full refunds

#### Industry Impact

**Market Shock**:
- Retailers lost millions in inventory
- Consumer confidence damaged
- Supply chain scrutiny intensified

**Regulatory Enhancement**:
- OCM strengthened facility inspections
- Strict supply chain document review
- Increased vigilance for "ghost brands"

**Retailer Pushback**:
- New York licensed retailers filed lawsuits
- Questioned OCM oversight inadequacy
- Demanded compensation and support

#### Lessons and Insights

**For Regulators**:
1. Need more frequent facility inspections
2. Strengthen supply chain traceability verification
3. Quickly identify and shut down violating operations

**For Producers/Processors**:
1. Licenses non-transferable or rentable
2. All products must be produced by licensed entity
3. Violation risks far exceed short-term gains

**For Retailers**:
1. Thoroughly vet suppliers
2. Verify actual product production location
3. Require complete supply chain documentation

**For Consumers**:
1. Purchase products from known licensed brands
2. Check product label completeness
3. Contact OCM for verification when in doubt

### Case 3: Pesticide Exceedance Recall (March 2025)

#### Background

**Products Involved**: 37 cannabis products

**Issue**: Products found to contain **unacceptable pesticide levels**

**Discovery Source**:
- Routine laboratory spot-checking
- Complies with Part 129 testing requirements

#### Health Risk

**Pesticide Types**: Insecticide and fungicide residues

**Potential Health Effects**:
- Acute: Nausea, headache, skin irritation
- Long-term: Possible nervous system effects

**High-Risk Populations**:
- Pregnant and breastfeeding women
- Children and adolescents
- Immunocompromised individuals

#### Recall Response

**OCM Actions**:
- Immediate recall order
- Press conference
- Social media alerts

**Retailer Execution**:
- Products removed within 24 hours
- Posted recall notices
- Notified known buyers

**Consumer Response**:
- Majority of products returned
- Small number of adverse reaction reports (minor symptoms)

#### Root Cause Analysis

**Cultivation Stage Issues**:
- Used unapproved pesticides
- Failed to comply with pre-harvest safety interval
- Incomplete record-keeping

**Corrective Measures**:
- Cultivators received additional training
- Enhanced pesticide use records
- Increased pre-harvest testing

#### Prevention Recommendations

**Cultivators**:
1. Only use OCM-approved pesticides
2. Strictly comply with application schedules
3. Detailed record-keeping of all pesticide use
4. Self-test before harvest

**Processors/Distributors**:
1. Require cultivators to provide pesticide use records
2. Conduct pre-receipt rapid testing
3. Work with reliable licensed laboratories

**Regulators**:
1. Publish approved pesticide list
2. Provide cultivator training resources
3. Random spot-checking of high-risk products

## 5. Compliance Checklists and Flowcharts

### 5.1 Product Recall Emergency Checklist

#### Producer/Processor Recall Checklist

**Phase 1: Within 2 Hours of Receiving Recall Notice**
- [ ] Designate recall coordinator
- [ ] Activate recall emergency plan
- [ ] Stop all affected product distribution
- [ ] Quarantine affected inventory in warehouse

**Phase 2: Within 6 Hours of Notice**
- [ ] Extract complete distribution records for affected products from Metrc system
- [ ] Generate list of all recipients (distributors, retailers)
- [ ] Prepare recall notice text
- [ ] Establish recall hotline

**Phase 3: Within 12 Hours of Notice**
- [ ] Contact all distributors and retailers by phone
- [ ] Send formal recall notice via email
- [ ] Confirm recipients have received notice
- [ ] Submit preliminary recall report to OCM

**Phase 4: Within 24 Hours of Notice**
- [ ] Verify all retailers have removed products from shelves
- [ ] Arrange product return logistics
- [ ] Prepare documents for OCM-supervised destruction
- [ ] Issue public statement to media (if OCM required)

**Phase 5: Ongoing (Until Recall Complete)**
- [ ] Daily update of product recovery quantities
- [ ] Submit weekly progress reports to OCM
- [ ] Track products not yet returned
- [ ] Conduct root cause analysis
- [ ] Develop Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPA)
- [ ] Complete OCM-supervised product destruction

#### Retailer Recall Checklist

**Immediately Upon Receiving Recall Notice**
- [ ] Verify recall notice authenticity (contact OCM)
- [ ] Mark affected products in POS system
- [ ] Remove all affected products from shelves
- [ ] Move products to quarantine area and label

**Within 1 Hour**
- [ ] Post recall notice in conspicuous in-store location
- [ ] Notify all employees not to sell affected products
- [ ] Report recall situation to management

**Within 4 Hours**
- [ ] Extract purchase records from sales system
- [ ] Generate buyer contact list (if records available)
- [ ] Prepare return processing procedure
- [ ] Train employees how to handle consumer inquiries

**Within 24 Hours**
- [ ] Begin notifying known buyers (phone/email/text)
- [ ] Document notification attempts and results
- [ ] Confirm product return procedure with supplier
- [ ] Report execution status to OCM (if required)

**Ongoing**
- [ ] Process consumer returns and refunds
- [ ] Record returned product quantities
- [ ] Track products not returned
- [ ] Cooperate with OCM or supplier follow-up investigations

### 5.2 Consumer Complaint Processing Checklist

#### Retailer Complaint Processing Checklist

**Upon Receiving Complaint**
- [ ] Listen politely to consumer concerns
- [ ] Record detailed information:
  - Consumer name and contact information
  - Product name, brand, batch number
  - Purchase date and location
  - Problem description (symptoms, defects, etc.)
- [ ] Check purchase records
- [ ] Check if product has active recall

**Initial Response (On-Site)**
- [ ] If recalled product: Immediate full refund
- [ ] If product defect: Offer exchange or refund
- [ ] If health issue: Recommend medical care and report to OCM
- [ ] Provide OCM complaint channel information

**Adverse Health Reaction (Special Procedure)**
- [ ] Recommend consumer seek immediate medical care (if symptoms serious)
- [ ] Retain product sample (if possible)
- [ ] Photo document product status
- [ ] Immediately report to OCM
- [ ] Report to supplier within 24 hours
- [ ] Document all communications and actions

**Follow-up**
- [ ] Contact consumer within 24-48 hours to confirm problem resolved
- [ ] Relay consumer feedback to supplier
- [ ] If batch issue discovered, check other products from same batch
- [ ] File and archive complaint and resolution records

**Reporting Standards**
Must report to OCM in following situations:
- [ ] Any adverse health reaction
- [ ] Multiple complaints about same product
- [ ] Product safety hazard
- [ ] Suspected product adulteration or contamination

## 6. Best Practices

### 6.1 Producer/Processor Best Practices

#### Quality Management System (QMS)

**Establish Robust QMS**:
1. **Documented Processes**
   - Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)
   - Batch production records
   - Testing and release procedures

2. **Supplier Management**
   - Review supplier qualifications and licenses
   - Supplier audit plan
   - Raw material acceptance testing

3. **Product Testing**
   - Work with multiple licensed laboratories
   - Mandatory testing before batch release
   - Retain test certificate copies

4. **Traceability System**
   - Complete Metrc records
   - Batch numbering system
   - Forward and backward traceability capability

#### Recall Preparation

**Recall Emergency Plan**:
1. **Designate Recall Team**
   - Recall coordinator (primary and backup)
   - Quality assurance director
   - Legal counsel
   - Public relations contact

2. **Contact Information Database**
   - All distributors and retailers
   - OCM contacts
   - Media contacts
   - Legal counsel

3. **Mock Recall Drills**
   - At least annually
   - Test notification system
   - Evaluate response time
   - Update procedures

4. **Recall Insurance**
   - Consider product liability insurance
   - Recall expense insurance
   - Business interruption insurance

### 6.2 Retailer Best Practices

#### Supplier Management

**Supplier Review Process**:
1. **Initial Review**
   - Verify OCM license validity
   - Check enforcement action history
   - Request quality management system documents

2. **Ongoing Oversight**
   - Regularly check license renewals
   - Monitor recall announcements
   - Evaluate product quality complaints

3. **Diversify Suppliers**
   - Don't over-rely on single supplier
   - Balance inventory distribution
   - Reduce recall risk

#### Customer Relationship Management

**Build Trust and Transparency**:
1. **Educational Materials**
   - Product usage guides
   - Dosage recommendations
   - Health and safety tips

2. **Purchase Record System** (Optional but Recommended)
   - Voluntary registration program
   - Rapid recall notification
   - Personalized service

3. **Complaint Handling Training**
   - Employee empathy training
   - Rapid resolution processes
   - Escalation procedures

4. **Customer Communication**
   - Regular newsletters (email/text)
   - Recall and safety information
   - New products and promotions

#### Inventory Management

**First-In, First-Out (FIFO)**:
1. **Expiration Date Tracking**
   - POS system integration
   - Automatic expiration alerts
   - Regular inventory reviews

2. **Slow-Moving Products**
   - Identify and promote
   - Avoid large inventory backlog
   - Reduce recall exposure

3. **Recall Quarantine Area**
   - Dedicated storage area
   - Clear identification
   - Controlled access

### 6.3 Consumer Best Practices

#### Safe Purchasing

**Smart Shopping Habits**:
1. **Only Purchase from Licensed Retailers**
   - Verify OCM license display
   - Avoid illegal sources
   - Use OCM retailer finder tool

2. **Check Product Labels**
   - OCM compliant labels
   - THC/CBD content
   - Batch number and expiration date
   - Testing laboratory information

3. **Retain Records**
   - Purchase receipts
   - Product packaging and labels
   - Batch number photos

#### Safe Use

**Responsible Consumption**:
1. **Start with Low Dose**
   - Especially new products
   - Wait for full effects
   - Gradually increase dose

2. **Read Instructions**
   - Recommended dosage
   - Usage method
   - Warnings and precautions

3. **Safe Storage**
   - Out of children's reach
   - Keep in original packaging
   - Appropriate temperature and humidity

#### Report Issues

**Active Participation**:
1. **Timely Reporting**
   - Adverse health reactions
   - Product quality issues
   - Suspected unlicensed operations

2. **Detailed Information**
   - Complete product information
   - Symptom or issue description
   - Photos and batch numbers

3. **Follow-up**
   - Maintain communication with OCM
   - Cooperate with investigations
   - Share experiences to educate others

## 8. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q1: How to check current product recalls?**
A: Visit OCM Recalls Page (https://cannabis.ny.gov/recalls), search all active and historical recalls by date, brand, product type.

**Q2: Can opened recalled products be returned?**
A: Yes. For recalled products, retailers **must accept returns** even if opened or partially used, and provide full refunds.

**Q3: What happens after reporting adverse health reaction?**
A: OCM will investigate report, may contact you for more information, and assess if regulatory action needed (such as product testing or recall). Your report helps protect public health.

**Q4: What if merchant refuses my reasonable return request?**
A: First, refer to posted in-store refund policy. If you believe merchant violated New York State refund laws, can file complaint with NYC DCWP (311) or NYS Consumer Protection Division (1-800-697-1220).

**Q5: What consequences for merchants not posting refund policy?**
A: Merchant must comply with default 30-day return policy (if consumer retains receipt and merchandise unused). Merchant may face DCWP fines and other penalties.

**Q6: What information should be on cannabis product labels?**
A: Compliant labels must include:
- Product name and type
- THC and CBD content (mg and %)
- Batch number
- Production and expiration dates
- Licensed producer information
- Testing laboratory and results
- Warning labels (pregnant women, driving, etc.)
- Allergen information

**Q7: How to verify if cannabis retailer is licensed?**
A: Use license lookup tool on OCM website (https://cannabis.ny.gov/licensing), or check license prominently displayed in store. Can call OCM or verify through 311.

**Q8: How long does complaint processing take?**
A: Depends on complaint type and severity:
- Emergency (health hazard): Initial response within 24 hours
- High priority (minor sales): 48 hours
- General complaints: 5-10 business days
- Complex investigations: 30-90 days

**Q9: Can consumers sue over recalled products?**
A: If recalled products cause health injury or economic loss, consumers may have legal claim rights. Recommend consulting product liability attorney to evaluate case.

**Q10: How to subscribe to OCM recall alerts?**
A: Visit OCM website to subscribe to newsletter, follow OCM social media accounts (Twitter, Facebook), or register for retailer consumer notification system.

## 9. Glossary

**CAPA (Corrective and Preventive Action)**: Corrective and preventive measures, procedures in quality management system to solve problems and prevent recurrence.

**CAURD (Conditional Adult-Use Retail Dispensary)**: Temporary adult-use retail dispensary, accelerated licensing path New York State provides for social equity applicants.

**Class I/II/III Recall**: Recall classification system, categorized by health risk severity (Class I most serious, Class III least).

**Labor Peace Agreement**: Agreement cannabis businesses sign with labor organizations, guaranteeing worker rights and peaceful labor-management relations.

**Metrc**: New York State mandatory cannabis tracking system, records all product movement from seed to sale.

**OCM (Office of Cannabis Management)**: New York State Office of Cannabis Management, state-level regulatory agency.

**Part 129**: New York State Cannabis Regulations Part 129, specifies product testing, marketing and advertising standards.

**Rent-a-License**: License renting, illegal practice where licensed entity allows unlicensed party to use its license for business.

**SEE (Social and Economic Equity)**: Social and Economic Equity certification, provides licensing benefits for historically disadvantaged groups.

**TPI (True Parties of Interest)**: True parties of interest, all individuals and entities with financial or control interest in cannabis business.
