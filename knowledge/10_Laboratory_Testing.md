## Part 130 Overview

### Scope
Part 130 establishes comprehensive standards for cannabis laboratory operations, including:
- Laboratory permit requirements and qualifications
- ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation standards
- Mandatory testing analytes and methods
- Sample collection and transport protocols
- Certificate of Analysis (COA) requirements
- Quality assurance and proficiency testing

### Regulatory Framework
All cannabis products destined for consumer sale must undergo complete laboratory testing by OCM-permitted facilities. Testing ensures product safety, potency accuracy, and contamination screening.

## ISO/IEC 17025 Accreditation

### Mandatory Accreditation Standard

**Requirement:**
All cannabis laboratories must maintain **ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation** or an accreditation based on ISO/IEC 17025 by a laboratory accreditation authority approved by OCM.

**Per 9 NYCRR § 130.7:**
Laboratories must demonstrate technical competence through ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation for all testing scopes listed on their permit.

### ISO/IEC 17025 Components

**Standard Requirements:**
1. **Management Requirements:**
   - Quality management system documentation
   - Document control procedures
   - Management review processes
   - Corrective and preventive actions
   - Internal audits
   - Management responsibility

2. **Technical Requirements:**
   - Personnel qualifications and competence
   - Accommodation and environmental conditions
   - Test methods and method validation
   - Equipment calibration and maintenance
   - Measurement traceability
   - Sampling procedures
   - Handling of test items
   - Quality assurance of results
   - Reporting of results

### Accreditation Assessment

**Third-Party Assessment Focus:**
- **Staff Expertise:** Technical competence and training documentation
- **Quality Assurance:** QA/QC protocols and implementation
- **Testing Methods:** Validation, verification, and standardization
- **Reporting Systems:** Accuracy, traceability, and documentation
- **Equipment Maintenance:** Calibration schedules and preventive maintenance
- **Facility Standards:** Environmental controls and safety protocols

**Approved Accreditation Bodies:**
- ANAB (ANSI National Accreditation Board)
- A2LA (American Association for Laboratory Accreditation)
- IAS (International Accreditation Service)
- Other ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation bodies approved by OCM

## Proficiency Testing Requirements

### Mandatory Proficiency Testing

**Per 9 NYCRR § 130.7:**
Cannabis laboratories must participate in proficiency tests by an **approved proficiency test provider** and pass **2 out of the most recent 3 proficiency tests** as part of permit requirements.

### Proficiency Testing Program

**Testing Frequency:**
- Regular participation in PT programs (typically quarterly)
- Proficiency tests for each analyte category
- Blind samples provided by PT providers

**Performance Standards:**
- Pass rate: 2 out of 3 most recent tests
- Failure triggers corrective action requirements
- Consecutive failures may result in permit suspension

**Approved PT Providers:**
- OCM maintains list of approved providers
- Providers must meet ISO/IEC 17043 standards
- Laboratory must document all PT participation

### Failure Response Protocol

**If PT Failure Occurs:**
1. Immediate investigation of root cause
2. Corrective action plan submitted to OCM
3. Implement corrective measures
4. Retest proficiency (if required)
5. Document all actions for OCM inspection

## Mandatory Testing Requirements

### Testing Scope per 9 NYCRR § 130.22

**All cannabis products and medical cannabis must be tested for:**

#### 1. Phytocannabinoid Profiles
- **Required Cannabinoids:**
  - THC (Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol)
  - THCA (Tetrahydrocannabinolic acid)
  - Total THC (THCA × 0.877 + THC)
  - CBD (Cannabidiol)
  - CBDA (Cannabidiolic acid)
  - Total CBD (CBDA × 0.877 + CBD)
  - CBN (Cannabinol)
  - CBG (Cannabigerol)

- **Testing Method:** High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC) or Gas Chromatography (GC)
- **Reporting:** mg per serving and mg per package

#### 2. Microbiological Contaminants
- **Required Tests:**
  - Total Yeast and Mold Count
  - Total Aerobic Microbial Count (TAMC)
  - Bile-Tolerant Gram-Negative Bacteria (BTGN)
  - *E. coli*
  - *Salmonella* spp.
  - *Aspergillus* spp. (*A. fumigatus, A. flavus, A. niger, A. terreus*)
  - *Shiga toxin-producing E. coli* (STEC)

- **Testing Method:** Culture-based or molecular methods (qPCR)
- **Action Limits:** Established by OCM (published on website)

#### 3. Heavy Metals
- **Required Metals:**
  - Arsenic (As)
  - Cadmium (Cd)
  - Lead (Pb)
  - Mercury (Hg)

- **Testing Method:** Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry (ICP-MS)
- **Action Limits:** Parts per billion (ppb) as established by OCM

#### 4. Pesticides
- **Testing Scope:** OCM-published list of required pesticides
- **Common Pesticides Tested:**
  - Over 50+ compounds including organophosphates, pyrethroids, neonicotinoids
  - Complete list available in OCM testing specifications

- **Testing Method:** Liquid Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) or Gas Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (GC-MS/MS)
- **Action Limits:** Parts per billion (ppb) as established by OCM

#### 5. Mycotoxins
- **Required Mycotoxins:**
  - Aflatoxin B1, B2, G1, G2
  - Ochratoxin A

- **Testing Method:** LC-MS/MS
- **Action Limits:** Parts per billion (ppb) as established by OCM

#### 6. Residual Solvents
- **Required Solvents:**
  - Butane
  - Propane
  - Ethanol
  - Isopropanol
  - Acetone
  - Hexane
  - Heptane
  - Methanol
  - And others used in extraction processes

- **Testing Method:** Gas Chromatography-Flame Ionization Detection (GC-FID) or Headspace GC-MS
- **Action Limits:** Parts per million (ppm) as established by OCM

#### 7. Moisture Content and Water Activity
- **Moisture Content:** Percentage of water by weight
- **Water Activity (aw):** Measure of free water available for microbial growth
- **Limits:** Water activity must be below 0.65 to prevent microbial growth
- **Testing Method:** Moisture analyzer and water activity meter

#### 8. Terpenoid Profile
- **Common Terpenes Tested:**
  - Myrcene
  - Limonene
  - Caryophyllene
  - Linalool
  - Pinene (alpha and beta)
  - Humulene
  - Terpinolene
  - And other strain-specific terpenes

- **Testing Method:** Gas Chromatography (GC-FID or GC-MS)
- **Reporting:** Percentage or mg/g

### Foreign Material Inspection

**Visual Inspection Required:**
- Presence of insects, hair, or other contamination
- Mold or discoloration
- Packaging integrity
- Product appearance and condition

## Testing Methods and Approval

### OCM-Approved Methods

**Method Approval Process:**
Per 9 NYCRR § 130.22, laboratories must use "methods approved by the Office" that fall "within the scope listed on the laboratory's current permit."

**Method Validation Requirements:**
- Demonstration of capability at testing site
- Validation studies for accuracy, precision, and sensitivity
- Method verification for new equipment or personnel
- Ongoing method performance monitoring

**Approved Testing Methods:**
OCM publishes and updates the list of approved testing methods on its website, including:
- Cannabis Analyte Testing Specifications
- Whole Flower Homogenization Procedures
- Sample Preparation Guidelines

### Laboratory Demonstration of Capability

**Before Analyzing Samples:**
Laboratories must conduct a "demonstration of capability" (DOC) at the testing site using approved methods.

**DOC Requirements:**
- Initial demonstration for new methods
- Demonstrate accuracy and precision
- Train all analysts performing the test
- Document all demonstration results
- Maintain records for OCM inspection

## Sample Collection and Transport

### Licensed Sampling Firms

**Sampling Requirements:**
- Only licensed sampling firms may collect product samples
- Samplers must follow OCM-approved sampling protocols
- Random and representative sampling required
- Maintain strict chain-of-custody

**Sampling Protocols:**
- Use sterile sampling tools
- Collect samples from each batch/lot
- Homogenize samples when required (whole flower)
- Package samples securely for transport
- Complete transport manifest

### Chain of Custody

**Required Documentation:**
- Sample identification (batch/lot number)
- Date and time of collection
- Sampler identification
- Transporting entity
- Temperature monitoring (if applicable)
- Seal integrity verification
- Laboratory receipt confirmation

**Transport Standards:**
- Secure, enclosed vehicles
- Temperature-controlled transport (for certain products)
- Direct route to laboratory
- No stops except laboratory delivery
- GPS tracking recommended

### Sample Integrity

**Storage Conditions:**
- Appropriate temperature and humidity
- Secure storage to prevent tampering
- Limited access to authorized personnel
- Tracking system integration

**Retention Requirements:**
- Laboratories must retain samples for specified period
- Reserve samples maintained for retesting
- Disposal procedures documented

## Certificate of Analysis (COA)

### COA Requirements

**Mandatory Issuance:**
Per 9 NYCRR § 130.22 and OCM guidance, laboratories must issue a Certificate of Analysis (COA) within **48 hours** of test completion.

**COA Recipients:**
- Entity that submitted the sample (confidentially)
- New York State Office of Cannabis Management (OCM)

### COA Content Requirements

**Mandatory Information:**

1. **Laboratory Information:**
   - Laboratory name and permit number
   - Contact information
   - ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation details
   - Laboratory director signature

2. **Sample Identification:**
   - Client name and license number
   - Product name and type
   - Batch/lot number
   - Sample collection date
   - Sample receipt date
   - Analysis completion date

3. **Testing Methods:**
   - Methods used for each analyte category
   - Reference to approved methods
   - Method detection limits (LOD) and quantification limits (LOQ)

4. **Analytical Results:**
   - **Cannabinoid Profile:** THC, CBD, CBN, and other cannabinoids (mg/serving, mg/package, percentage)
   - **Microbiological Results:** Pass/Fail for each organism
   - **Heavy Metals:** Concentration in ppb with pass/fail status
   - **Pesticides:** Detected pesticides with concentrations and pass/fail
   - **Mycotoxins:** Concentration in ppb with pass/fail
   - **Residual Solvents:** Concentration in ppm with pass/fail
   - **Moisture and Water Activity:** Percentage and aw value
   - **Terpenoid Profile:** Terpene concentrations

5. **Pass/Fail Determination:**
   - Overall batch status: PASS or FAIL
   - Individual analyte category pass/fail status
   - Reference to OCM action limits

6. **Quality Assurance:**
   - QA/QC measures employed
   - Uncertainty of measurement (if applicable)
   - Disclaimers and limitations
   - Statement of accreditation

### COA Interpretation

**Understanding Results:**

**PASS Status:**
- All analytes meet OCM action limits
- Product may proceed to retail sale
- COA must be available to consumers (QR code or upon request)

**FAIL Status:**
- One or more analytes exceed action limits
- Product cannot be sold to consumers
- Remediation or disposal required
- OCM notification mandatory

**Common Failure Scenarios:**
- High yeast/mold counts indicating improper storage
- Pesticide detection above limits
- Heavy metal contamination (soil or water source)
- Residual solvent levels exceeding limits (extraction issue)

### COA Access for Consumers

**Transparency Requirements:**
- QR code on product packaging linking to COA
- COA available upon request at dispensary
- Digital COA database (OCM may establish)
- COA must be easily readable and understandable

## Laboratory Quality Systems

### Quality Assurance/Quality Control (QA/QC)

**OCM Laboratory Quality System Standard (Updated 12/8/2025):**

**Required QA/QC Elements:**

1. **Method Blanks:**
   - Run with each analytical batch
   - Detect contamination or carryover

2. **Laboratory Control Samples (LCS):**
   - Known concentration standards
   - Verify method accuracy

3. **Matrix Spike/Matrix Spike Duplicate:**
   - Assess matrix interference
   - Evaluate precision and accuracy

4. **Duplicate Samples:**
   - Assess analytical precision
   - Run at specified frequency

5. **Calibration Standards:**
   - Multi-point calibration curves
   - Regular verification and recalibration
   - Traceable reference standards

6. **Positive and Negative Controls:**
   - For microbiological testing
   - Verify media and method performance

**Acceptance Criteria:**
- All QC samples must meet established criteria
- Failures trigger investigation and corrective action
- QC data documented and available for inspection

### Equipment Calibration and Maintenance

**Calibration Requirements:**
- Initial calibration before use
- Regular calibration verification
- Recalibration after repairs or adjustments
- Calibration standards traceable to national standards

**Maintenance Programs:**
- Preventive maintenance schedules
- Repair documentation
- Performance verification after maintenance
- Equipment logbooks

### Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)

**Required SOPs:**
- Sample receipt and handling
- Testing methods for each analyte
- Equipment operation and maintenance
- Calibration procedures
- QA/QC protocols
- Data review and approval
- COA generation and issuance
- Corrective action procedures
- Personnel training
- Chain of custody procedures

**SOP Management:**
- Version control and change tracking
- Regular review and updates
- Staff training on updated SOPs
- Accessible to all laboratory personnel

## Remediation and Retesting

### Failed Batch Procedures

**Upon Batch Failure:**
1. Laboratory issues failing COA
2. OCM and licensee notified
3. Product quarantined (cannot be sold)
4. Licensee evaluates remediation options

### Remediation Options

**Permitted Remediation:**
- **Reprocessing:** Extract cannabinoids and retest
- **Dilution:** Blend with passing batches (if potency issue only)
- **Removal of Contaminated Portion:** If contamination isolated

**Prohibited:**
- Sale of failed products to consumers
- Relabeling without retesting
- Mixing failed and passed batches to "average" results

### Retesting Requirements

**After Remediation:**
- Full analyte panel retesting required
- Same laboratory or different OCM-permitted lab
- New COA issued with new batch number
- OCM approval may be required for certain remediations

**Retest Protocols:**
- Different sample from same batch
- Same laboratory using same methods
- Investigation if significant variance between tests

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1: Can a dispensary operate its own testing laboratory?
**A:** No, cannabis testing must be conducted by independent, OCM-permitted laboratories. Vertical integration does not extend to laboratory testing to ensure impartial results.

### Q2: How long does laboratory testing take?
**A:** Typically 7-14 business days from sample receipt to COA issuance, depending on laboratory workload and testing complexity. COA must be issued within 48 hours after test completion.

### Q3: What happens if a product fails testing?
**A:** The product cannot be sold. Options include remediation (reprocessing, extraction) and retesting, or disposal. OCM must be notified of all failed batches.

### Q4: Can I use out-of-state laboratories?
**A:** No, only OCM-permitted laboratories in New York State may test cannabis products for New York dispensaries.

### Q5: Is testing required for every batch?
**A:** Yes, every batch of cannabis product destined for retail sale must undergo complete laboratory testing and receive a passing COA.

### Q6: Can consumers request to see COA before purchase?
**A:** Yes, dispensaries must make COAs available to consumers upon request or via QR code on packaging.

### Q7: What if two labs give different results?
**A:** Variance can occur due to sample heterogeneity or method differences. A third lab may be used for adjudication. OCM may investigate significant discrepancies.
