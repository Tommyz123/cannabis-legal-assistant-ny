## Part 129 Overview

### Scope
Part 129 establishes comprehensive restrictions on cannabis marketing and advertising to:
- Prevent appeal to individuals under 21 years of age
- Prohibit false or misleading health claims
- Restrict location-based advertising near youth facilities
- Ensure responsible marketing practices

### Regulatory Framework
All cannabis licensees must comply with Part 129 marketing restrictions, including:
- Adult-use retail dispensaries
- Cultivators and processors
- Distributors and delivery services
- Registered organizations

---

## Prohibited Content and Practices

### 1. Youth Appeal Prohibitions

**Prohibited Imagery and Design:**
- Products designed to be attractive to individuals under twenty-one
- Cartoon characters or voices
- Children's voices or audio appealing to minors
- Bright colors or designs targeting youth
- Games, toys, or youth-oriented themes

**Enforcement Standard:**
All marketing materials must pass the "youth appeal test" - would a reasonable person consider this content appealing to minors?

### 2. Prohibited Slang Terms

**Banned Colloquial References:**
The following terms are **prohibited** in advertising and marketing materials:
- "Stoner"
- "Chronic"
- "Weed"
- "Pot"
- "Sticky buds"

**Exception:** These terms may be used if they appear in:
- The licensee's legal entity name
- Registered "Doing Business As" (DBA) name
- Approved logo filed with OCM
- Business name approved during licensing

### 3. Health and Safety Claims Prohibitions

**Strictly Prohibited Statements:**
- Cannabis has "curative or therapeutic effects"
- Products are "safe" because they are regulated by the Board/Office
- False health claims or unsubstantiated benefits
- Medical symbols suggesting medicinal status
- Claims that cannabis treats or prevents any disease or condition

**Prohibited Terms:**
- "Organic" (unless USDA certified, which is not available for cannabis)
- "Natural medicine"
- "Therapeutic"
- "Healing"

### 4. Potency and Concentration Prohibitions

**Banned Marketing Practices:**
- Promoting product potency or high THC concentration
- Advertising THC percentage as a selling point
- "Strongest," "most potent," or similar competitive claims
- Marketing based on intoxication level

**Compliance Note:**
THC content must be disclosed on labels for consumer safety, but cannot be used as a marketing advantage.

## Location and Audience Restrictions

### Geographic Advertising Restrictions

**500-Foot Prohibition Zone:**
Advertisements are **prohibited** within 500 feet of:
- Schools (K-12 and higher education)
- Recreation centers and youth facilities
- Childcare facilities and daycares
- Playgrounds
- Public parks
- Public libraries

**Measurement Method:**
Distance measured from property line to property line via the shortest pedestrian walking route.

**Enforcement:**
Licensees are responsible for verifying location restrictions before placing any outdoor advertising.

### Audience Demographic Requirements

**90% Legal Drinking Age (LDA) Threshold:**
All advertising must demonstrate that **at least 90%** of the audience is 21 years of age or older.

**Applies to:**
- Social media advertising campaigns
- Digital marketing platforms
- Print publications
- Television and radio advertisements
- Sponsored content
- Event marketing

**Verification Requirements:**
- Use platform analytics showing age demographics
- Maintain documentation for OCM inspection
- Age-gate all digital content
- Implement geo-targeting to exclude restricted areas

## Prohibited Advertising Formats

### Billboard Advertising

**Status:** Prohibited as of February 24, 2026

**Transition Requirements:**
- All existing billboards must be removed by February 24, 2026
- Prohibited signage must be taken down by compliance deadline
- No new billboard advertising permitted

**Exception:**
Signage on the licensed premises (following Part 129.4 rules) is permitted.

### Distribution and Promotional Methods

**Prohibited Practices:**
- Handbills distributed in public areas or parking lots
- Unsolicited promotional materials
- Pop-up advertisements on non-age-verified websites
- Banner ads outside age-restricted platforms
- Commercial mascots promoting cannabis products

### Product Depictions

**Strictly Prohibited in All Advertising:**
- Depiction of cannabis flower or products
- Images showing cannabis consumption
- Action of smoking or vaping
- Any visual representation of product use
- Paraphernalia or consumption devices

## Promotional Activities and Pricing

### Price Promotions and Discounts

**2026 Regulatory Update:**
New York has authorized certain promotional activities that were previously restricted.

**Permitted (as of 2026):**
- Loyalty programs and rewards systems
- Point-based customer incentive programs
- Responsible discount programs
- Price promotions that do not encourage overconsumption

**Prohibited:**
- "Buy One, Get Five Free" or bulk incentives
- Promotions encouraging excessive purchasing
- Free giveaways of cannabis products
- Promotional merchandise with cannabis references

### Loyalty Programs

**Best Practices for Compliance:**
- Structure rewards to provide value without encouraging overconsumption
- Implement purchase limits per transaction
- Maintain customer privacy and data security
- Age-verify all program participants
- Track and report program metrics to OCM

## Permitted Marketing Strategies

### Educational Content

**Allowed Content Types:**
- Evidence-based information about cannabis products
- Safe use practices and consumption guidelines
- Product differentiation based on strain or type
- Responsible consumption education
- General wellness information (without medical claims)

### Brand Building

**Permitted Approaches:**
- Highlighting dispensary mission and values
- Commitment to quality and safety
- Community involvement and social responsibility
- Professional branding and visual identity
- Customer service excellence

### Digital Marketing

**Age-Gated Platforms:**
Advertising permitted on platforms where licensees can verify 90%+ audience is 21+:
- Email marketing to verified customers
- Age-gated websites
- Age-restricted social media advertising
- Search engine marketing with age verification
- Geotargeted mobile advertising (excluding restricted zones)

**Social Media Best Practices:**
- Use platform age-verification tools
- Implement content warnings
- Monitor audience demographics regularly
- Respond professionally to customer inquiries
- Avoid viral marketing that may reach youth audiences

## Signage Requirements and Restrictions

### Exterior Signage (Limited)

**Permitted Information Only:**
- Business name
- Business address
- Contact information (phone, website)
- Hours of operation

**Prohibited Elements:**
- Images of cannabis or cannabis products
- Slang terms or colloquial references
- Bright colors designed to attract attention
- Animated or electronic displays
- Size exceeding local zoning requirements

### Interior Signage (Mandatory)

**Required Legal Notices:**
- Health warning statements
- Possession limits (3 oz flower, 24g concentrate)
- Consumption prohibitions (no public consumption)
- Age verification notice (21+ only)
- Return and exchange policy
- Patient privacy notices (for dual-use facilities)

**Format Requirements:**
- Clearly visible to all customers
- Minimum font size for readability
- Posted at point of sale
- Available in multiple languages if serving non-English speakers

## Enforcement and Penalties

### OCM Inspection Authority

**Inspection Scope:**
- Review all advertising and marketing materials
- Verify audience demographic data
- Inspect signage for compliance
- Examine promotional program structures
- Request documentation of marketing spend and targeting

### Violation Consequences

**Potential Penalties:**
- Written warning for first minor violation
- Civil penalties ranging from $500 to $10,000 per violation
- Mandatory corrective action plan
- Suspension of license for repeated violations
- Revocation of license for egregious violations

**Aggravating Factors:**
- Marketing that directly targets youth
- False health claims
- Repeated violations after warnings
- Failure to comply with corrective action plans

## Case Studies and Practical Examples

### Example 1: Compliant Social Media Campaign

**Scenario:**
A dispensary creates an Instagram campaign promoting a new product line.

**Compliance Measures:**
- Use Instagram's age-restriction settings (21+)
- Verify analytics show 95% of followers are 21+
- Content focuses on product education without health claims
- No images of cannabis products, only branded graphics
- Includes responsible use messaging
- Geo-targeted to exclude 500-foot restricted zones

**Result:** Compliant campaign

### Example 2: Non-Compliant Billboard

**Scenario:**
A dispensary maintains a billboard advertisement near a highway.

**Violation:**
- Billboard advertising prohibited as of February 24, 2026
- Located within 500 feet of a public park

**Corrective Action:**
- Remove billboard by compliance deadline
- Redirect marketing budget to digital age-gated platforms

### Example 3: Loyalty Program Review

**Scenario:**
A dispensary implements a points-based rewards program.

**Compliance Assessment:**
- ✓ Points earned at reasonable rate (1 point per $1 spent)
- ✓ Rewards redeemable for discounts, not free products
- ✓ Purchase limits maintained (3 oz maximum)
- ✓ Program does not incentivize bulk purchases
- ✓ Customer data securely stored

**Result:** Compliant program structure

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1: Can I use the word "marijuana" in my advertising?
**A:** Yes, "marijuana" and "cannabis" are acceptable terms. The prohibition applies to slang terms like "weed," "pot," "chronic," etc. (unless in your business name).

### Q2: Can I advertise on Facebook or Instagram?
**A:** Yes, but only with strict age-gating and audience verification showing 90%+ users are 21+. Use platform analytics to demonstrate compliance.

### Q3: Can I sponsor local community events?
**A:** Yes, if the event audience is at least 90% adults 21+ and the venue is not within 500 feet of restricted locations. Avoid youth-oriented events.

### Q4: Can I give away branded t-shirts or merchandise?
**A:** No, promotional merchandise bearing cannabis references is prohibited. You may give away non-cannabis branded items (e.g., store logo without cannabis imagery).

### Q5: Can I advertise medical benefits if they're scientifically proven?
**A:** No, claims that cannabis has "curative or therapeutic effects" are strictly prohibited, even if supported by research.

### Q6: What if my business name includes a prohibited slang term?
**A:** If the term appears in your OCM-approved business name or logo, you may use it. This is the only exception to the slang term prohibition.
